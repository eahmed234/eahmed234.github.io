# Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī (بناء المسجد النبوي الشريف)
*The building of the Prophet's ﷺ greatest mosque and of the chambers of his wives*

The Prophet's ﷺ mosque in [[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah]] was built on a *mirbad* — a place where dates are put to dry — that belonged to two orphans, Sahl and Suhayl. His she-camel had knelt at its door, and he said: «This is the dwelling-place, if Allāh wills.»[^s3.335] He bargained for it with [[Banū al-Najjār]], refused to accept it as a gift, and bought it; then he built on it, working with the Companions with his own hands.[^s3.335][^s3.336] Two houses were also built for his wives.[^s3.338]

## The site

The Ṣaḥīḥ reports that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ sent to the nobles of Banū al-Najjār about the site of the mosque and said: «O Banū al-Najjār, name me a price for this garden of yours.» They answered: "By Allāh, we ask its price only from Allāh."[^bukh428][^musl524] According to the report of the two orphans, he called the boys and bargained with them over the *mirbad*, and they said, "Rather we give it to you, O Messenger of Allāh"; he refused to accept it from them as a gift until he had bought it, and then built it as a mosque.[^s3.335]

[[Asʿad b. Zurārah]] had already made the *mirbad* into a mosque before the Prophet ﷺ arrived. Al-Nawwār bint Mālik, the mother of Zayd b. Thābit, is reported to have seen him lead the people in the five prayers and the Friday prayer there; she said that when the Prophet ﷺ came he prayed with them in that mosque and built it, "so it is his mosque."[^s3.335]

Who the two orphans' father was is disputed. Yaḥyā b. al-Ḥasan, al-Balādhurī and others say they were the sons of Rāfiʿ b. Abī ʿAmr b. ʿĀʾidh b. Thaʿlabah b. Ghanm b. Mālik b. al-Najjār, and Ibn Ḥazm states this expressly; Mūsā b. ʿUqbah has "Rāfiʿ b. ʿAmr b. Abī ʿAmr", while al-Zuhrī and Ibn Isḥāq say they were the sons of ʿAmr, which the author of *al-ʿUyūn* calls the better known. Al-Suhaylī tried to reconcile the views by saying "Rāfiʿ b. ʿAmr", so that they are ascribed to their grandfather; al-Ḥāfiẓ judged the view of al-Zuhrī and Ibn Isḥāq the more probable.[^s3.335][^s3.343]

They were in the guardianship (*ḥijr*) of Asʿad b. Zurārah according to most narrators of al-Bukhārī. Ibn Isḥāq says they were with Muʿādh b. ʿAfrāʾ; Ibn Zabālah and Yaḥyā say with [[Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī]]. Al-Ṣāliḥī notes that the reports may be reconciled by those named sharing in the guardianship, or by its passing from one to another after Asʿad; and Ibn Abī Fudayk heard some scholars say that Asʿad died before the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ built the mosque.[^s3.335][^s3.343]

## Clearing and first building

Anas reports that there were palm trees, graves of the polytheists and ruins on the ground. The Prophet ﷺ ordered the palm trees cut down, the graves dug up and the ruins levelled;[^bukh428][^musl524] the bones were ordered to be hidden, and water that stood in the *mirbad* was drained away.[^s3.335] The palm trees were set in a row as the *qiblah*, and the jambs of the door were made of stone.[^bukh428][^musl524] Anas describes the mosque as a wall with no roof, with its *qiblah* toward al-Quds.[^s3.335]

Ibn ʿĀʾidh reports that the Prophet ﷺ prayed in it for twelve days while it was a *ʿarīsh* (a shelter of light roofing), and that it was then roofed. Shahr b. Ḥawshab reports that he said: «Build me a *ʿarīsh* like the *ʿarīsh* of Mūsā — some *thumām* and some timbers and a shade like the shade of Mūsā — and the matter is quicker than that.»[^s3.335]

## Dimensions and materials

The reports differ on the size. Khārijah b. Zayd b. Thābit says the mosque was seventy by sixty cubits or more, the bricks made from Baqīʿ al-Khabkhabah, its pillars timber, with a *raḥbah* (open court) in the middle.[^s3.338] Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad reports that it was built of single courses of brick (*sumayṭ*), then of brick and a half, and that when the people grew many they asked for it to be enlarged and it was, with two different kinds of brick (*al-dhakar wa-l-unthā*); its foundation was raised to nearly three cubits with stones; and its length from the *qiblah* side to the rear was a hundred cubits, and likewise its breadth, being square. Before it was roofed its wall stood a man's height and something more. It was not made flat-roofed, so the people complained of the heat; they made its pillars of trunks and shaded it with palm-ribs, then with woven leaf, and when it dripped on them they plastered it with clay.[^s3.338]

Ibn ʿUmar says that in the time of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ the mosque was built of adobe, its roof palm-ribs and its columns palm timber.[^bukh446] Al-Zubayr b. Bakkār reports from Anas that he built it at first with palm-ribs and with adobe only four years after the Hijrah; and Ibn Zabālah reports from Jaʿfar that the Prophet ﷺ built it twice — at less than a hundred by a hundred when he arrived, and again, after Allāh gave him victory at [[Ghazwat Khaybar|Khaybar]], adding to it the like of it in circumference.[^s3.338]

Usāmah b. Zayd reports from his father [[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]] that the Prophet ﷺ made its *qiblah* toward Bayt al-Maqdis and made three doors for it at its rear: the door of Abū Bakr, which is on the *qiblah* side today; the door of ʿĀtikah, also called Bāb al-Raḥmah; and the door by which the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ used to enter, which is today the door of Āl ʿUthmān. The last two were not changed when the *qiblah* was turned; at that time the Prophet ﷺ closed the door behind him and opened the one opposite it.[^s3.338] Another report, from Ibn ʿUmar and several others, says that Jibrīl told him to set the *qiblah* while looking at the Kaʿbah, and every mountain between them moved aside; when he had finished, Jibrīl restored them. The Prophet ﷺ is reported to have said: «I did not set the *qiblah* of this mosque of mine until the Kaʿbah was raised for me and I set it in front of it.» Imām Mālik said only: "I have heard that Jibrīl is the one who set up the *qiblah* of the mosque of Madinah."[^s3.339]

## The work

The Prophet ﷺ began to carry bricks with the Companions, to encourage them to work, saying: «O Allāh, the reward is the reward of the Hereafter, so have mercy on the Anṣār and the Muhājirah.»[^s3.336] Al-Bukhārī and Muslim give the verse as a plea to forgive or help the Anṣār and Muhājirūn, as the people chanted *rajaz* with him.[^bukh428][^musl524] It is mentioned that the verse is by [[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]; al-Zuhrī reports the Prophet ﷺ as saying it in different words, and adds that he did not keep verse straight.[^s3.336]

Umm Salamah reports that when the first Muhājirūn and the Anṣār saw him lay down his cloak they threw off theirs and worked, chanting: "If we sit while the Prophet works, that would then be misguided work." Al-Ḥasan reports that he took bricks with them until his chest was dust-covered.[^s3.336]

The same report has [[ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn]], a man given to fastidiousness, shaking out his sleeve and brushing the dust from his garment after laying a brick. [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]] recited: "Not alike are he who builds the mosques, toiling in them standing and sitting, and he who is seen turning aside from the dust." [[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]] heard it and began to chant it, not knowing whom it meant; An exchange followed in which he was addressed as "son of Sumayyah" — "how well I know whom you are alluding to" — and a threat was made to strike his face with a palm-rib. The Prophet ﷺ was angry and said that ʿAmmār is «the skin between my eyes and my nose». The people held back from him; ʿAmmār said they wanted to kill him — they carried one brick each and loaded him with two — and the Prophet ﷺ took him by the hand, went around the mosque with him, wiped the dust from his hair and said: «They are not the ones who will kill you; the rebellious party will kill you — you call them to Paradise and they call you to the Fire.»[^s3.336]

In the report of Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī, ʿAmmār carried two bricks, one for himself and one for the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and the Prophet ﷺ wiped his back and said: «O son of Sumayyah, the people have one reward and you two; your last provision will be a drink of milk; and the rebellious party will kill you.»[^s3.337] In al-Bukhārī's own wording Abū Saʿīd says: "We were carrying one brick at a time and ʿAmmār two at a time; the Prophet ﷺ saw him and was brushing the dust from him, saying: «Woe to ʿAmmār — he calls them to Paradise and they call him to the Fire.»" The words "the rebellious party will kill him" stand in the printed text only within editorial brackets, as an addition in some narrators' versions.[^bukh447] Al-Ṣāliḥī, following al-Sayyid, places the saying in the second building, since al-Bayhaqī reports ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr recalling it to his father ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ, whose Islam al-Samhūdī dates to the fifth year of the Hijrah.[^s3.344]

Other Companions are named at the work. [[Abū Hurayrah]] offered to take a brick the Prophet ﷺ held across his belly, and he answered: «Take another; there is no life but the life of the Hereafter» — which al-Ṣāliḥī says belongs to the second building, since Abū Hurayrah had not yet become Muslim at the first. [[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]] asked for a stone and was told: «Go and carry another, for you are not more in need of Allāh than I.»[^s3.337] Ṭalq b. ʿAlī, who was skilled at mixing clay, took the spade; the Prophet ﷺ said: «This Ḥanafī is a man of clay,» and: «Bring the Yamāmī close to the clay, for he is the best of you at handling it and the strongest of you in the shoulder.»[^s3.337]

## The first stones and the added plot

ʿĀʾishah reports that when the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ laid the foundation he brought a stone and set it, then [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]], [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]] and [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]] each brought a stone and set it; asked about it, he said: «This is the matter of the caliphate after me.» Abū Yaʿlā reports it with the men of the Ṣaḥīḥ, except that the *tābiʿī* in the chain did not hear from ʿĀʾishah; al-Bayhaqī has the like from Safīnah with a strong, good chain, with the words «These are the holders of authority after me.»[^s3.337]

Al-Ṭabarānī reports from Abū al-Malīḥ that the Prophet ﷺ told the Anṣārī owner of a plot that was added to the mosque: «You will have a house in Paradise for it.» ʿUthmān bought it from him for ten thousand dirhams and sold it to the Prophet ﷺ for a house in Paradise; the Prophet ﷺ set a brick, then Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān each set one, and he told the people to set theirs.[^s3.338][^s3.339] In another report ʿUthmān, on the day of the house (*yawm al-dār*), recalls the Prophet's ﷺ saying «Whoever buys the plot of Banū so-and-so and adds it to the mosque will have better than it in Paradise»; he says he bought it with his own wealth for twenty thousand and the Prophet ﷺ replied: «Put it in our mosque, and yours is its reward.» Al-Tirmidhī graded it *ḥasan*.[^s3.339]

## The price of the site

The reports on payment do not agree, and al-Ṣāliḥī sets them out with his reconciliations, which he hedges. According to Ibn ʿUyaynah's report, the boys' uncle was asked to buy it and, learning that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ wanted it, the two said "We give it", and gave it to him. Ibn Zabālah and Yaḥyā report that Abū Ayyūb said: "I shall satisfy the two of them." Ibn ʿUqbah mentions that Asʿad compensated them with palm trees, and says it is also said that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ bought it from them. It is also reported that the two refused any compensation.[^s3.344]

Al-Wāqidī says he bought it from Banū ʿAfrāʾ for ten gold dinars paid by Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq;[^s3.344][^musl524b] al-Ṣāliḥī suggests that Abū Bakr perhaps paid the ten along with the others, or that the Prophet ﷺ took part of the *mirbad* in the first building and another part later, the price coming from Abū Bakr's money in one and from the others in the other. He also says it is possible that those who said "we ask its price only from Allāh" bore the price for the boys.[^s3.344]

## The chambers of his wives

The author of *al-Rawḍ* says that his houses were nine, some of palm-ribs plastered with clay and some of piled stones, all roofed with palm-ribs. Al-Dhahabī doubts that nine houses were built when the mosque was built: he says that only one, for Sawdah, was wanted, and that no other was needed until he built one for ʿĀʾishah in Shawwāl of the year 2, the rest being built at different times.[^s3.348] Khārijah b. Zayd reports that two houses were built for his two wives.[^s3.338] Al-Ṣāliḥī says the two were [[Sawdah bint Zamʿah]] and [[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]], in the manner of the building of the mosque, and that the rest of the chambers were built as they were needed.[^s3.348] Al-Wāqidī reports that Ḥārithah b. al-Nuʿmān had houses near and around the mosque and, each time the Prophet ﷺ took a new wife, gave up one to him, until they were all the Messenger of Allāh's ﷺ and his wives'.[^s3.348]

When ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz demolished the houses on the order of al-Walīd b. ʿAbd al-Malik, ʿAbdullāh b. Yazīd al-Hudhalī saw them: houses of adobe with chambers of palm-ribs plastered with clay, nine in all; he saw that Umm Salamah's chamber was of adobe. Her grandson told him that she had built it when the Prophet ﷺ was at [[Ghazwat Dūmat al-Jandal|Dūmat al-Jandal]]; on his return he asked what the building was, she said she wanted to keep people's eyes off, and he said: «O Umm Salamah, the worst thing a Muslim's money goes on is building.»[^s3.348] ʿImrān b. Abī Anas says there were four houses of adobe with palm-rib chambers and five of plastered palm-ribs with no chambers, with hair curtains on their doors. Muḥammad b. Hilāl says none stood on the west of the mosque, and that ʿĀʾishah's door faced Syria and had a single leaf, of juniper or teak.[^s3.348][^s3.349] Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, Dāwūd b. Qays and Bishr b. Ṣiḥār each say that they could reach the roofs by hand or saw the chambers covered with hair curtains.[^s3.349]

ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī was present when al-Walīd's letter was read ordering the chambers' demolition and says he never saw a day with more weeping. Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab said: "By Allāh, I wish they had left them as they were, so that a youth of Madinah might grow up and a newcomer from the horizons arrive and see what the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was content with in his life." ʿImrān b. Abī Anas saw Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf, Abū Umāmah b. Sahl b. Ḥunayf and Khārijah b. Zayd b. Thābit weeping until their beards were wet; Abū Umāmah said he wished they had been left, so that people would see what Allāh was content with for His Prophet while the keys of the world's treasuries were in his hand.[^s3.348][^s3.349]

According to ʿAmr b. Dīnār and ʿUbaydullāh b. Abī Marthad there was no wall round his house in the Prophet's ﷺ time; ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb first built one, short, and ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr later built it up.[^s3.349] It is reported that the Companions knocked at his door with their fingernails out of courtesy and reverence; it is also said that his door had no rings to knock with, and al-Suhaylī preferred the first.[^s3.349][^s3.350]

## Later changes

Ibn ʿUmar reports that the columns decayed in the caliphate of Abū Bakr, who rebuilt with palm trunks and ribs without adding; that ʿUmar added to it and rebuilt it as in the Prophet's ﷺ time, with adobe and ribs, restoring the columns in timber; and that ʿUthmān added a great deal, building the walls of carved stone and *qaṣṣah* and roofing it with teak.[^s3.339][^bukh446] Al-Walīd b. ʿAbd al-Malik later ordered ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, his governor in Madinah, to demolish and rebuild the mosque, in the year 87 or, it is said, 88.[^s3.340]
