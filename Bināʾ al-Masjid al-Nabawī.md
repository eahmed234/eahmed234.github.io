# Bināʾ al-Masjid al-Nabawī (بناء المسجد النبوي الشريف)
*The building of the Prophet's ﷺ greatest mosque and of the chambers of his wives*

The Prophet's ﷺ mosque in [[al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah]] was built on a *mirbad* — a place where dates are put — that belonged to two orphans, Sahl and Suhayl. His she-camel had knelt at its door, and he said: «This is the dwelling-place, if Allāh wills.»[^s3.335] He asked [[Banū al-Najjār]] for it, would not take it as a gift, and bought it; he then worked on the building with the Companions with his own hands.[^s3.335][^s3.336] Two houses were built for his two wives.[^s3.338]

## The site

The Ṣaḥīḥ reports that the Prophet ﷺ sent to the nobles of Banū al-Najjār about the site and said: «O Banū al-Najjār, name me a price for this garden of yours.» They answered: "By Allāh, we ask its price only from Allāh."[^bukh428][^musl524] In another report he called the two boys and bargained with them over the *mirbad* so as to take it as a mosque; they said, "Rather we give it to you, O Messenger of Allāh," but he refused to accept it as a gift until he had bought it, and then he built it as a mosque.[^s3.335]

[[Asʿad b. Zurārah]] had already made the *mirbad* into a mosque before the Prophet ﷺ arrived. Al-Nawwār bint Mālik, the mother of [[Zayd b. Thābit]], is reported to have seen him leading the people there in the five prayers and the Friday prayer; she said of the Prophet ﷺ: "When he arrived he prayed with them in that mosque and built it, so it is his mosque."[^s3.335]

The sources disagree about the boys' father and their guardian, and about who paid. Yaḥyā b. al-Ḥasan, al-Balādhurī and others say they were the sons of Rāfiʿ b. Abī ʿAmr b. ʿĀʾidh b. Thaʿlabah b. Ghanm b. Mālik b. al-Najjār, and Ibn Ḥazm states this expressly.[^s3.335] Mūsā b. ʿUqbah has them as the sons of Rāfiʿ b. ʿAmr b. Abī ʿAmr, while al-Zuhrī and Ibn Isḥāq say they were the sons of ʿAmr; the author of *al-ʿUyūn* calls the latter the better known, and al-Ḥāfiẓ judged it the more probable. Al-Suhaylī tried to reconcile the two by saying "Rāfiʿ b. ʿAmr", so that the boys are ascribed to their grandfather.[^s3.343]

According to most narrators of al-Bukhārī, they were in the guardianship (*ḥijr*) of Asʿad b. Zurārah; Abū Dharr al-Harawī read the name with the alif, "Asʿad", which al-Ḥāfiẓ and al-Sayyid call the correct reading. Ibn Isḥāq mentions that they were in the care of Muʿādh b. ʿAfrāʾ, and Ibn Zabālah and Yaḥyā that they were in the care of [[Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī]]. It may be reconciled by those named having shared in it, or by its passing from one to another after Asʿad. Ibn Abī Fudayk heard some of the people of knowledge say that Asʿad died before the Prophet ﷺ built the mosque.[^s3.335][^s3.343]

On the price, Ibn ʿUyaynah's report says that the Prophet ﷺ spoke to the boys' uncle — the one in whose care they were — about buying it from them, and the two said, "We give it," and gave it to him, and he built it. Ibn Zabālah and Yaḥyā mention that Abū Ayyūb said: "O Messenger of Allāh, I shall satisfy the two of them." Ibn ʿUqbah mentions that Asʿad compensated them with palm trees — a grove of his in Bayāḍah — and it is said that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ bought it from them. It is also reported that the two refused compensation.[^s3.344] Al-Wāqidī says that he bought it from Banū ʿAfrāʾ for ten gold dinars, which Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq paid.[^s3.344][^musl524b] The accounts may be reconciled, it is suggested, in several ways: perhaps those who said "we ask its price only from Allāh" bore the price for the boys; perhaps Asʿad, Abū Ayyūb and Ibn ʿAfrāʾ each satisfied them with something, which was then ascribed to each; perhaps Abū Bakr, desiring the good, paid the ten along with those; or perhaps the Prophet ﷺ took part of the *mirbad* in his first building, in the year of his arrival, and another part later, the price coming from Abū Bakr's money in one and from the others in the other.[^s3.344]

## Clearing the ground and the first building

Anas reports that the ground held palm trees, the graves of the polytheists and ruins. The Prophet ﷺ ordered the graves dug up, the ruins levelled and the palm trees cut down; the palm trees were set in a row as the *qiblah* of the mosque, and its two jambs were made of stone.[^bukh428][^musl524] The bones were ordered to be hidden, and water that stood in the *mirbad* was drained away.[^s3.335] Anas describes the mosque as a wall with no roof, its *qiblah* toward al-Quds; the palm trees became pillars on the *qiblah* side and it was roofed upon them.[^s3.335] Ibn ʿĀʾidh reports that the Prophet ﷺ prayed in it for twelve days while it was a *ʿarīsh* (a roof, a shade), and then it was roofed. Shahr b. Ḥawshab reports that when he wished to build the mosque he said: «Build me a *ʿarīsh* like the *ʿarīsh* of Mūsā — some *thumām* and some timbers and a shade like the shade of Mūsā — and the matter is quicker than that.» Asked what the shade of Mūsā was, he said that when one stood up it touched one's head.[^s3.335][^s3.336]

## The work

The Prophet ﷺ worked with the Companions in the building himself; as in the Ṣaḥīḥ, he began to carry the adobe bricks with them, al-Ṣāliḥī adding that he did so to encourage them.[^s3.336] They carried rock, chanting *rajaz* with him among them, and he said: "O Allāh, there is no good but the good of the Hereafter, so forgive the Anṣār and the Muhājirah" — in Muslim, "so help".[^bukh428][^musl524] Another wording is: "O Allāh, the reward is the reward of the Hereafter, so have mercy on the Anṣār and the Muhājirah." It is mentioned that this verse is by [[ʿAbdullāh b. Rawāḥah]]; al-Zuhrī reports the Prophet ﷺ as saying it in other words and adds that he did not keep verse straight.[^s3.336]

Umm Salamah reports that he brought the bricks near, and stood and put down his cloak; when the first Muhājirūn and the Anṣār saw it they threw off their cloaks and mantles and began to work, chanting: "If we sit while the Prophet works, that would then be misguided work."[^s3.336] Al-Ḥasan says the Companions helped him as he took the bricks with them, until his chest was dust-covered.[^s3.336]

In al-Ḥasan's report, [[ʿUthmān b. Maẓʿūn]], a man given to *tanaṭṭuʿ* (fastidiousness), would shake out his sleeve after setting a brick down and brush off any dust that touched his garment. [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]] looked at him and recited: "Not alike are he who builds the mosques, toiling in them standing and sitting, and he who is seen turning aside from the dust." [[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]] heard it and began to chant it, not knowing whom it meant, and passed ʿUthmān, and there followed the words "O son of Sumayyah, how well I know whom you are alluding to" and, with a palm-rib in hand, "You will stop, or I shall strike your face with it." The Prophet ﷺ heard it and was angry. He said: «ʿAmmār b. Yāsir is the skin between my eyes and my nose, and when it reaches that point with a man, it has gone far,» and put his hand between his eyes. The people held back from ʿAmmār, then told him they feared that Qurʾān would come down about them; ʿAmmār said he would please the Prophet ﷺ as he had been angry, and said, "They want to kill me; they carry one brick each, and they load me with two." The Prophet ﷺ took him by the hand and went around the mosque with him, wiping the dust from his hair, and said: «O son of Sumayyah, they are not the ones who will kill you; the rebellious party will kill you — you call them to Paradise and they call you to the Fire.» ʿAmmār said: "I seek refuge with Allāh from the trials."[^s3.336]

Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī's report has the Companions carrying one brick each and ʿAmmār two, one for himself and one for the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ; the Prophet ﷺ wiped his back and said: «O son of Sumayyah, the people have one reward and you two; your last provision will be a drink of milk; and the rebellious party will kill you — you call them to Paradise and they call you to the Fire.»[^s3.337] In al-Bukhārī's own text Abū Saʿīd says: "We were carrying one brick at a time and ʿAmmār two at a time; the Prophet ﷺ saw him and was brushing the dust from him, saying: «Woe to ʿAmmār — he calls them to Paradise and they call him to the Fire.»" The words "the rebellious party will kill him" stand in the printed text only within editorial brackets, as an addition in some narrators' versions, and are not in the narration of Abū Dharr and al-Aṣīlī.[^bukh447] Al-Sayyid mentions that the saying to ʿAmmār belongs to the second building, because al-Bayhaqī reports from Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī that ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. al-ʿĀṣ said it to his father ʿAmr; al-Samhūdī adds that this implies the second building, since ʿAmr's Islam was in the fifth year of the Hijrah.[^s3.344]

Others are named at the work. [[Abū Hurayrah]] met the Prophet ﷺ holding a brick across his belly and, thinking it burdened him, offered to take it; he said: «Take another; there is no life but the life of the Hereafter.» Al-Ṣāliḥī infers that this was in the second building, since Abū Hurayrah had not yet become Muslim at the first.[^s3.337] [[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]] met him as he came out with a stone and asked for it, and he answered: «Go and carry another, for you are not more in need of Allāh than I.»[^s3.337] Ṭalq b. ʿAlī, a man of labour and mixing clay, took the spade and mixed the clay; the Prophet ﷺ was looking at him and saying: «This Ḥanafī is a man of clay,» and: «Bring the Yamāmī close to the clay, for he is the best of you at handling it and the strongest of you in the shoulder.»[^s3.337]

## The first stones and the added plot

ʿĀʾishah reports that when the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ laid the foundation he brought a stone and set it, and [[Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq]], [[ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb]] and [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]] each brought one and set it; asked about it, he said: «This is the matter of the caliphate after me.» Abū Yaʿlā reports it with the men of the Ṣaḥīḥ, except that the *tābiʿī* in the chain did not hear from ʿĀʾishah. Al-Bayhaqī reports the like from Safīnah, freedman of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, with a strong, good chain, in which he said: «These are the holders of authority after me.»[^s3.337]

Al-Ṭabarānī reports from Abū al-Malīḥ that the Prophet ﷺ said to the owner of the plot that was added to the mosque, one of the Anṣār: «You will have a house in Paradise for it.» ʿUthmān came and said, "You shall have ten thousand dirhams for it," and bought it; then he came to the Prophet ﷺ and asked him to buy it from him, and he bought it for a house in Paradise. ʿUthmān said: "I bought it for ten thousand dirhams." The Prophet ﷺ then set a brick, called Abū Bakr, who set a brick, then ʿUmar, then ʿUthmān, and told the people to set theirs.[^s3.338][^s3.339] In another report, ʿUthmān, on the day of the house, adjures the people by Allāh and recalls the Prophet's ﷺ words: «Whoever buys the plot of Banū so-and-so and adds it to the mosque will have better than it in Paradise.» He says that he bought it from his own wealth for twenty thousand and told the Prophet ﷺ, who said: «Put it in our mosque, and yours is its reward.» Aḥmad and al-Tirmidhī report it, al-Tirmidhī grading it *ḥasan*.[^s3.339]

## Size, materials, doors and *qiblah*

Khārijah b. Zayd b. Thābit says that he built his mosque seventy by sixty cubits or more; that he made bricks from Baqīʿ al-Khabkhabah and made it a wall, its pillars timber, plank by plank, with a *raḥbah* (the courtyard of the mosque) in the middle; and that he built two houses for his two wives.[^s3.338] Jaʿfar b. Muḥammad reports that it was built first by *sumayṭ*, one brick upon another, then by *saʿīd*, a brick and a half of another. When the people became many they said, "O Messenger of Allāh, if only it were enlarged," and he did so, building with *al-dhakar wa-l-unthā*, two different bricks. They had raised its foundation with stones to nearly three cubits, and made its length from the *qiblah* side to its rear a hundred cubits, and likewise its breadth, so that it was square. It was not flat-roofed, so they complained of the heat; they made its pillars of trunks and shaded it with palm-ribs, then with *khaṣaf* (woven palm-leaf), and when it dripped on them they plastered it with clay. Before it was roofed its wall was a man's height and something.[^s3.338]

Ibn Zabālah reports from Jaʿfar that the Prophet ﷺ built the mosque twice: when he arrived, at less than a hundred by a hundred, and when Allāh gave him victory at [[Ghazwat Khaybar|Khaybar]], adding to it the like of it in circumference. Al-Zubayr b. Bakkār reports from Anas that he built it at first with palm-ribs, and built it with adobe only four years after the Hijrah.[^s3.338] According to Ibn ʿUmar, in the Prophet's ﷺ time the mosque was of adobe, its roof palm-ribs and its columns palm timber.[^bukh446]

Usāmah b. Zayd reports from [[Zayd b. Ḥārithah]] that the Prophet ﷺ made its *qiblah* toward Bayt al-Maqdis and made three doors for it at its rear: the door of Abū Bakr, which is today on the *qiblah* side; the door of ʿĀtikah, called Bāb ʿĀtikah and also Bāb al-Raḥmah; and the door by which he used to enter, today the door of Āl ʿUthmān. The last two were not changed after the *qiblah* was turned; when it was turned the Prophet ﷺ closed the door that was behind him and opened the one opposite it.[^s3.338]

Ibn ʿUmar is the source of a report, with many named transmitters, that the Prophet ﷺ stationed a group at the corners of the mosque to straighten the *qiblah*, and Jibrīl came to him and said: «O Messenger of Allāh, set the *qiblah* while you are looking at the Kaʿbah.» He gestured with his hand and every mountain between the Prophet ﷺ and the Kaʿbah moved aside, so that he laid out the squaring of the mosque while looking at it; when he had finished, Jibrīl gestured again and the mountains, trees and things were restored, and the *qiblah* came to be toward the *mīzāb*. The Prophet ﷺ said: «I did not set the *qiblah* of this mosque of mine until the Kaʿbah was raised for me and I set it in front of it.» Imām Mālik, as in *al-ʿUtbiyyah*, said: "I have heard that Jibrīl is the one who set up the *qiblah* of the mosque of Madinah for the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ."[^s3.339]

## The chambers of his wives

Al-Suhaylī in *al-Rawḍ* says that his houses were nine, some of palm-ribs plastered with clay and some of piled stones, all roofed with palm-ribs. Al-Dhahabī doubts that nine houses were built when the mosque was: he says he does not think so, that only one was wanted, for Sawdah, and that no other was needed until he built one for ʿĀʾishah in Shawwāl of the year 2, the rest being built at different times.[^s3.348] He built for his two wives, [[Sawdah bint Zamʿah]] and [[ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr]], in the manner of the building of the mosque, because ʿĀʾishah was already his wife though the consummation was delayed, and then built the rest of the chambers when they were needed.[^s3.348] Al-Wāqidī reports that Ḥārithah b. al-Nuʿmān had houses near the mosque and around it, and each time the Prophet ﷺ took a new wife gave up a house to him, until they were all the Messenger of Allāh's ﷺ and his wives'.[^s3.348]

ʿAbdullāh b. Yazīd al-Hudhalī, who saw the houses when ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz demolished them on the order of al-Walīd b. ʿAbd al-Malik, says they were houses of adobe with chambers of palm-ribs plastered with clay, nine in all, and that Umm Salamah's chamber was of adobe. He was told that she built it when the Prophet ﷺ campaigned at [[Ghazwat Dūmat al-Jandal|Dūmat al-Jandal]]; on his return he looked at the adobe, entering upon her first of his wives, and asked, «What is this building?» She said, "I wanted, O Messenger of Allāh, to keep people's eyes off," and he said: «O Umm Salamah, the worst thing a Muslim's money goes on is building.»[^s3.348] ʿImrān b. Abī Anas says there were four houses of adobe with palm-rib chambers and five of plastered palm-ribs with no chambers, with hair curtains on their doors.[^s3.349] Muḥammad b. Hilāl says that none of them stood on the west of the mosque and that ʿĀʾishah's door faced Syria and had a single leaf, of juniper or teak.[^s3.349] Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī and Bishr b. Ṣiḥār each say they used to enter the houses and reach the roof with the hand, and Dāwūd b. Qays saw the palm-rib chambers covered on the outside with hair curtains.[^s3.349]

ʿAṭāʾ al-Khurāsānī, who knew the chambers, was present when al-Walīd's letter ordering their demolition was read, and says he never saw a day with more weeping.[^s3.348] Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab said that day: "By Allāh, I wish they had left them as they were, so that a youth of Madinah might grow up and a newcomer from the horizons arrive and see what the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was content with in his life, and that would make people abstain from boasting and rivalry in wealth."[^s3.348][^s3.349] ʿImrān saw Abū Salamah b. ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf, Abū Umāmah b. Sahl b. Ḥunayf and Khārijah b. Zayd b. Thābit weeping until they wet their beards; Abū Umāmah said he wished they had been left, so that people might desist from building and see what Allāh was content with for His Prophet while the keys of the treasuries of the world were in his hand.[^s3.349]

ʿAmr b. Dīnār and ʿUbaydullāh b. Abī Marthad say that there was no wall round his house in the Prophet's ﷺ time; ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb was the first to build one, and it was short until ʿAbdullāh b. al-Zubayr built it.[^s3.349] The Companions used to knock at his door with their fingernails out of courtesy and reverence; it is also said that his door had no rings to knock with, and al-Suhaylī held the first preferable.[^s3.349][^s3.350]

## Later changes

According to Ibn ʿUmar, the columns decayed in the caliphate of Abū Bakr, who rebuilt with palm trunks and ribs and did not add to it; ʿUmar added to it and rebuilt it on its building in the Prophet's ﷺ time, with adobe and palm-ribs, restoring its columns as timber; and ʿUthmān made a large addition, building the wall of carved stones and *qaṣṣah* and making the columns of stone.[^s3.339][^s3.340][^bukh446] Al-Walīd b. ʿAbd al-Malik wrote to ʿUmar b. ʿAbd al-ʿAzīz, his governor over Madinah, ordering him to demolish the mosque and rebuild it; he rebuilt it and added to it, and put Ṣāliḥ b. Kaysān in charge of it and its expenditure. That was in the year 87, and it is said from the year 88.[^s3.340]
