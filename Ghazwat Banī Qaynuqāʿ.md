# Ghazwat Banī Qaynuqāʿ (غزوة بني قينقاع)
*The expedition against Banū Qaynuqāʿ, the first of the Jews to break the covenant*

The **Ghazwat Banī Qaynuqāʿ** was the expedition of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ against [[Banū Qaynuqāʿ]], the people of [[ʿAbdullāh b. Salām]], on a Saturday in mid-Shawwāl, twenty months from his hijrah.[^s4.179] They were goldsmiths and the bravest of the Jews, and were allies of [[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]], [[ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit]] and others of their people.[^s4.179] They were besieged for fifteen nights, surrendered to the Messenger's ﷺ judgment, and were expelled from Madinah.[^s4.180]

## Background

After the hijrah the disbelievers stood towards the Prophet ﷺ in three divisions. With one he made a truce on condition that they would neither fight him nor support his enemy against him: the three Jewish groups, Qurayẓah, al-Naḍīr and Banū Qaynuqāʿ. A second fought him and set up enmity against him: [[Quraysh]]. A third left him alone and waited to see what his affair would come to, such as groups of the Arabs; some of these inwardly wished for his triumph, like [[Banū Khuzāʿah]], and the reverse, like Banū Bakr; and some were outwardly with him and with his enemy, the hypocrites.[^s4.179]

When he came to Madinah, all the Jews made a truce with him. He wrote a document between himself and them, attached each people to their allies, made a security between himself and them, and stipulated conditions, among them that they would not aid an enemy against him.[^s4.179] When the day of [[Ghazwat Badr al-Kubrā|Badr]] came, Banū Qaynuqāʿ were the first of the Jews to break the covenant; they showed oppression and envy and cut what covenant there was between them and the Messenger ﷺ.[^s4.179]

## The call to Islam

The Prophet ﷺ gathered them in the market of Banū Qaynuqāʿ and said: «يا معشر يهود أسلموا، فو الله إنكم لتعلمون إني رسول الله صلى الله عليه وآله وسلم، يا معشر يهود احذروا من الله مثل ما نزل بقريش من النقمة فأسلموا، فإنكم قد عرفتم أني مرسل، تجدون ذلك في كتابكم وعهد الله إليكم». They replied that he thought them like his own people, that it should not deceive him that he had met a people with no knowledge of war and got an opportunity against them, and that if he fought them he would know that they were the men.[^s4.179] Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄ said, in what Ibn Isḥāq related, that these verses of [[Sūrat Āl ʿImrān]] (3:12–13) were revealed only about them.[^s4.179]

## The woman in the market

While they were in this state of showing enmity and casting off the covenant, a woman of the Arabs came with goods of hers, sold them in the market of Banū Qaynuqāʿ, and sat by a goldsmith there for some jewellery. They kept wanting her to uncover her face, and she would not. The goldsmith fastened the edge of her garment from behind with a thorn while she was unaware, and when she stood her *ʿawrah* appeared; they laughed at her, and she cried out. A man of the Muslims leapt on the goldsmith, who was a Jew, and killed him. The Jews fell on the Muslim and killed him, and cast the covenant back to the Prophet ﷺ. The Muslim's family called the Muslims to their aid against the Jews, the Muslims were angry, and evil broke out between them and Banū Qaynuqāʿ.[^s4.179]

Allāh revealed: «And if you fear treachery from a people, throw back to them on equal terms; Allāh does not love the treacherous» (al-Anfāl 8:58).[^s4.179] The Prophet ﷺ said: «إنما أخاف من بني قينقاع», and marched against them for this verse.[^s4.179][^s4.180]

## The siege

Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib ﵁ carried the banner, which was white that day; Ibn Saʿd said that the *rāyāt* did not exist then. He left [[Abū Lubābah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir]] ﵁ as deputy over Madinah.[^s4.180] Banū Qaynuqāʿ fortified themselves in their fortress and he besieged them with the severest siege; they stayed so for fifteen nights, until Allāh cast terror into their hearts and they surrendered to the judgment of the Messenger ﷺ, on the terms that their property was the Messenger's and that the women and children were theirs. He ordered them bound and put [[al-Mundhir b. Qudāmah]] al-Sulamī ﵁ in charge of binding them.[^s4.180]

## ʿUbādah and Ibn Ubayy

ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit ﵁ went to the Messenger ﷺ. Banū Qaynuqāʿ had of his alliance the like of what they had of that of ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy; he committed them to the Messenger ﷺ and disavowed to Allāh and His Messenger their alliance, saying: «يا رسول الله: أتولى الله ورسوله والمؤمنين وأبرأ من حلف هؤلاء الرجال».[^s4.180]

Ibn Ubayy rose to the Messenger ﷺ when Allāh had given him power over them and said: «يا محمد أحسن في موالي» — they were allies of the Khazraj. The Messenger ﷺ was slow to answer; Ibn Ubayy repeated it, and the Messenger ﷺ turned away from him. He put his hand into the collar-opening of the Messenger's ﷺ coat of mail from behind; it was called Dhāt al-Fuḍūl. The Messenger ﷺ said to him: «ويحك أرسلني», and was angry until they saw shades on his face (*ẓulal*, used figuratively for the darkening of his face when his anger became severe),[^s4.180][^s4.181] and said again: «ويحك أرسلني». Ibn Ubayy said: «والله لا أرسلك حتى تحسن في موالي: أربعمائة حاسر، وثلاثمائة دارع، قد منعوني من الأحمر والأسود، تحصدهم في غداة واحدة، إني والله امرؤ أخشى الدوائر». *Ḥāsir* here is one who has no coat of mail; *dārīʿ* one who has one on.[^s4.180][^s4.181] The Prophet ﷺ said: «خلوهم لعنهم الله ولعنه معهم».[^s4.180]

## Expulsion and spoils

He spared them from killing and ordered that they be expelled from Madinah; they went out after three. ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit ﵁ took charge of their expulsion, and it is said that it was Muḥammad b. Maslamah ﵁. They joined Adhriʿāt, a town in al-Shām; their stay there was short.[^s4.180][^s4.181]

The Messenger ﷺ took of their weapons three bows, called al-Katūm (which was broken at [[Ghazwat Uḥud|Uḥud]]), al-Rawḥāʾ and al-Bayḍāʾ; two coats of mail, called al-Ṣughdiyyah and Fiḍḍah; three spears; and three swords, one a Qalʿī sword, one called Battār, and one unnamed. In their dwellings much weaponry and goldsmith's equipment was found. He took his *ṣafī* and the fifth and distributed four-fifths of it among his Companions; it was the first fifth after Badr. The one who took possession of their property was [[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]] ﵁.[^s4.180]

## Revelation

Allāh revealed concerning ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy and ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit the verses of [[Sūrat al-Māʾidah]] beginning «O you who believe, do not take the Jews and Christians as allies» (5:51). The words «you see those in whose hearts is sickness… saying: we fear a turn of fortune may strike us» (5:52) refer to Ibn Ubayy and his saying «I fear the turns of fortune»; and «Your ally is only Allāh, His Messenger and those who believe» (5:55–56) is for ʿUbādah's taking Allāh, His Messenger and the believers as allies and disavowing Banū Qaynuqāʿ, their alliance and their guardianship.[^s4.180][^s4.181]

## Disputes

Al-Ḥākim stated that the expulsion of Banū Qaynuqāʿ and that of Banū al-Naḍīr were at one time, and he was not agreed with. The expulsion of al-Naḍīr was six months after Badr on the view of ʿUrwah, or a long time later on the view of Ibn Isḥāq, who placed it after Biʾr Maʿūnah in year four; the story of Banū Qaynuqāʿ was in mid-Shawwāl of year two.[^s4.181]
