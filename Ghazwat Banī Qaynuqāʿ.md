# Ghazwat Banī Qaynuqāʿ (غزوة بني قينقاع)
*The expedition against the Jewish goldsmiths of Banū Qaynuqāʿ, who broke the covenant and were expelled from Medina*[^s4.180]

The expedition against [[Banū Qaynuqāʿ]] took place on a Saturday in the middle of Shawwāl, twenty months from the Prophet's ﷺ hijrah.[^s4.179] They were the people of [[ʿAbdullāh b. Salām]], goldsmiths, the bravest of the Jews, and allies of [[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]] and [[ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit]].[^s4.179] When the day of Badr came they were the first of the Jews to break the covenant; the Prophet ﷺ besieged them for fifteen nights, they submitted to his judgement, and they were expelled from Medina.[^s4.179][^s4.180]

## Background

After the hijrah the unbelievers stood toward the Prophet ﷺ in three divisions: those with whom he made a truce, that they would neither fight him nor side with his enemy against him, who were the three groups of the Jews — Qurayẓah, al-Naḍīr and Banū Qaynuqāʿ; those who fought him and set enmity against him, who were [[Quraysh]]; and those who left him alone and waited to see where his affair would lead, as did groups of the Arabs. Among the last, some inwardly loved his prevailing, like [[Banū Khuzāʿah]], and the reverse was true of Banū Bakr; others were with him outwardly and with his enemy, and these were the hypocrites.[^s4.179]

When he came to Medina as an emigrant all the Jews made a truce with him. He wrote a document between himself and them, attached each people to their allies, set a security between them, and laid conditions on them, among them that they would not back an enemy against him.[^s4.179]

## The breach of the covenant

When the day of Badr came, Banū Qaynuqāʿ were the first of the Jews to break the covenant. They showed insolence and envy and severed what lay between them and the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ.[^s4.179] He gathered them in the market of Banū Qaynuqāʿ and said: «يا معشر يهود أسلموا، فو الله إنكم لتعلمون إني رسول الله». He told them to beware lest there descend on them from God the like of the retribution that had befallen Quraysh, and to embrace Islam, "for you have recognised that I am sent; you find that in your Book and in the covenant God made with you."[^s4.179] They answered: "O Muḥammad, you think we are like your people. Do not let it deceive you that you met a people who have no knowledge of war, and took an opportunity against them. By God, if you fight us you will surely know that we are the men."[^s4.179]

Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄ said, in what Ibn Isḥāq related, that the verses «قل للذين كفروا ستغلبون وتحشرون إلى جهنم وبئس المهاد» and what follows them (Qurʾān 3:12–13) were revealed only about them.[^s4.179]

## The incident in the market

While they were showing enmity and repudiating the covenant, a woman of the Arabs came with goods (*jalb*) of hers, sold in the market of Banū Qaynuqāʿ, and sat down beside a goldsmith there. They kept seeking to make her uncover her face and she would not. The goldsmith went to the hem of her garment from behind her and fastened it with a thorn while she did not notice, and when she stood up her *ʿawrah* was exposed and they laughed at her. She cried out, and a man of the Muslims leapt on the goldsmith, who was a Jew, and killed him. The Jews charged at the Muslim and killed him, and repudiated the covenant with the Prophet ﷺ. The Muslim's people called on the Muslims for help against the Jews; the Muslims grew angry and hostility broke out between them and Banū Qaynuqāʿ.[^s4.179][^s4.181]

God revealed: «وإما تخافن من قوم خيانة فانبذ إليهم على سواء إن الله لا يحب الخائنين» (Qurʾān 8:58). The Prophet ﷺ said: «إنما أخاف من بني قينقاع», and the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ set out toward them for this verse.[^s4.179][^s4.180]

## The siege

Ḥamzah b. ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib ﵁ carried the Prophet's ﷺ banner, which was white that day. Ibn Saʿd said that the *rāyāt* (banners) were not in use that day. The Prophet ﷺ appointed Abū Lubābah b. ʿAbd al-Mundhir ﵁ his deputy over Medina.[^s4.180]

Banū Qaynuqāʿ took refuge in their fortress and he besieged them with the severest siege. They remained so for fifteen nights, until God cast terror into their hearts, and they submitted to the judgement of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ on terms that their property was his and that the women and the offspring were theirs. He ordered that they be bound, and appointed al-Mundhir b. Qudāmah al-Sulamī ﵁ over their binding.[^s4.180]

## The intercession

ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit ﵁ went to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ. The Banū Qaynuqāʿ had from his alliance the like of what they had from ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl, and he placed them at the disposal of the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and disavowed to God and His Messenger their alliance. He said: "O Messenger of Allāh, I take God, His Messenger and the believers as my allies, and I disown the alliance of these men."[^s4.180]

Then ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl came up to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, when God had given him power over them, and said: "O Muḥammad, treat my clients well" — they were allies of the Khazraj. The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was slow to answer him and turned away. Ibn Ubayy put his hand into the collar of the Prophet's ﷺ coat of mail, called Dhāt al-Fuḍūl, from behind him. The Prophet ﷺ said: «ويحك أرسلني», and grew angry until they saw *ẓulal* ("shades", in origin clouds) on his face. He said again: «ويحك أرسلني». Ibn Ubayy said: "By God, I will not release you until you treat my clients well. Four hundred unarmoured and three hundred armoured; they have protected me from the red and the black, and you would reap them in a single morning. By God, I am a man who fears the reversals." The Prophet ﷺ said: «خلوهم لعنهم الله ولعنه معهم», and spared them from killing.[^s4.180][^s4.181]

## The expulsion

He ordered that they be expelled from Medina, and they went out after three. ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit ﵁ was in charge of their expulsion; it is said that it was Muḥammad b. Maslamah ﵁. They went to Adhriʿāt, a town in al-Shām, and their stay there was very short.[^s4.180][^s4.181]

## The spoils

The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ took three bows from their weapons — al-Katūm, which was broken at Uḥud, al-Rawḥāʾ and al-Bayḍāʾ — and two coats of mail, one called al-Ṣughdiyyah and another of *fiḍḍah*, three spears, and three swords: a Qalʿī sword, one called Battār, and another that is not named. Much weaponry and goldsmith's equipment was found in their dwellings. He took his *ṣafī* and the *khumus* and divided the four-fifths among his Companions; it was the first *khumus* after Badr. The one who took their property into his hand was Muḥammad b. Maslamah.[^s4.180]

## The revelation about Ibn Ubayy and ʿUbādah

God then revealed, concerning ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy and ʿUbādah b. al-Ṣāmit, Qurʾān 5:51: «يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تتخذوا اليهود والنصارى أولياء بعضهم أولياء بعض، ومن يتولهم منكم فإنه منهم، إن الله لا يهدي القوم الظالمين». The words «فترى الذين في قلوبهم مرض» (5:52) refer to Ibn Ubayy, and to his saying "I fear the reversals"; and the verse «إنما وليكم الله ورسوله والذين آمنوا» (5:55) was about ʿUbādah's allegiance to God, His Messenger and the believers and his disavowal of Banū Qaynuqāʿ, their alliance and their guardianship. The passage goes on: «ومن يتول الله ورسوله والذين آمنوا فإن حزب الله هم الغالبون» (5:56).[^s4.180][^s4.181]

## Disputes

**The date of the expulsion of Banū al-Naḍīr.** Al-Bayhaqī, and al-Bukhārī before him, mentioned the report of Banū al-Naḍīr before the battle of Uḥud. Ibn Kathīr's verdict is that the right course is to mention it after Uḥud, as Ibn Isḥāq and other imams of the *maghāzī* mentioned.[^s4.181] Al-Ḥākim stated that the expulsion of Banū Qaynuqāʿ and that of Banū al-Naḍīr were at one time, and this was not agreed to: the expulsion of Banū al-Naḍīr was six months after Badr on the view of ʿUrwah, as al-Bukhārī suspended it from him and ʿAbd al-Razzāq connected it, or a long time after that on the view of Ibn Isḥāq, who mentioned that it was after Bi'r Maʿūnah in the year four; whereas the story of Banū Qaynuqāʿ was in the middle of Shawwāl in the year two.[^s4.181]
