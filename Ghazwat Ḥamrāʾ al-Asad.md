# Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad (غزوة حمراء الأسد)
*The expedition to Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, in pursuit of Quraysh after Uḥud*

The **Ghazwat Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad** was the expedition on which the Prophet ﷺ led out those who had fought at [[Ghazwat Uḥud]] in pursuit of Abū Sufyān and the polytheists. Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad lies eight miles from Madinah, on the left of the road for one heading to Dhū al-Ḥulayfah.[^s4.313] He went out on the morning of Sunday, the day after he had returned from Uḥud.[^s4.308] He stayed there on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday and then went back.[^s4.312]

## The call to arms

The Prophet ﷺ returned from Uḥud on Saturday, and that night the leading men of the Aws and Khazraj stayed at his door, in fear of a return attack by the enemy. At dawn on Sunday Bilāl called the prayer and sat waiting for the Prophet ﷺ. ʿAbdullāh b. ʿAmr b. ʿAwf al-Muzanī then came and told him that, coming from his family, he had reached Malal, a place on the road to Makkah, and found Quraysh encamped there; he heard Abū Sufyān and his companions saying that they had struck the strength of the people but had left them undestroyed, that leaders remained among them who would gather against them, and that they should go back and wipe out the remainder. Ṣafwān b. Umayyah refused this, saying that the people had been enraged, that he feared those who had stayed behind would gather against them, and that they should return while the upper hand was theirs.[^s4.308]

The Prophet ﷺ said: «Ṣafwān guided them, yet he was not one to be guided; by Him in whose hand is my soul, stones were marked for them, and had they returned it would have been like yesterday that has gone.»[^s4.308] He called Abū Bakr and ʿUmar ﵄ and told them what al-Muzanī had said; they replied that he should pursue the enemy, lest they burst in upon the offspring.[^s4.308] When he finished the dawn prayer he called the people and ordered Bilāl to proclaim that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ commanded them to pursue their enemy and that none should go out with them except one who had been present at the fighting the day before.[^s4.308]

Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr, who had nine wounds and had wanted to treat them, said on hearing the call, «We hear and obey, to Allāh and His Messenger,» and did not stop for his wounds. Forty wounded men of Banū Salimah went out, among them al-Ṭufayl b. al-Nuʿmān with thirteen wounds, Khirāsh b. al-Ṣimmah with ten, Kaʿb b. Mālik with ten-odd (*biḍʿata ʿashara*) and Quṭbah b. ʿĀmir with nine; the Muslims sprang to their weapons and did not stop to treat their wounds.[^s4.308][^s4.309]

## Departure

ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy came and said that he would ride with the Prophet ﷺ; he answered, «No.» Other men who had not attended the fighting asked his permission and he refused them.[^s4.309] Jābir b. ʿAbdillāh ﵁ came and said that he had been eager to attend, but that his father had left him in charge of his seven sisters. His father had told him that it was not fitting for either of them to leave the women with no man, that he would not prefer Jābir to himself for *jihād* with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, and that Jābir should stay with them while he himself went out, hoping that Allāh would grant him martyrdom. Jābir had stayed behind, and his father had been given the martyrdom in preference to him; so he asked leave to march, and the Prophet ﷺ gave it. Jābir said that no one who had not been present at the fighting went out with him except himself.[^s4.309]

The Prophet ﷺ called for his banner, which was tied and had not been untied since the day before, and gave it to ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib ﵁; it is said that he gave it to Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq ﵁. He left Ibn Umm Maktūm ﵁ as his deputy over Madinah.[^s4.309] He went out wounded in his face from the two rings of the helmet, split in the forehead at the roots of the hair, with his *rabāʿiyah* tooth chipped and his lower lip wounded on the inside; his right shoulder was weakened by the blow of Ibn Qamiʾah and his knees were scraped.[^s4.309] He entered the mosque and prayed two *rakʿah*s while the people assembled, then called for his horse al-Sakb at the door of the mosque; at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad none of his Companions had a horse except his.[^s4.309]

Ṭalḥah b. ʿUbaydillāh ﵁, having heard the crier, went out to see when the Prophet ﷺ would march, and met him in coat of mail and helmet with nothing of him visible but his eyes. The Prophet ﷺ asked, «Ṭalḥah, where is your weapon?» Ṭalḥah said it was near, went and brought it; he had nine wounds in his chest, and said, «I was more concerned about the Messenger of Allāh's wounds than about my own.» The Prophet ﷺ asked where he thought the people were now, and Ṭalḥah answered that they were at al-Sayyālah, a village twenty-nine miles from Madinah. The Prophet ﷺ said, «That is what I thought. Know, Ṭalḥah, that they will not get the like of it from us until Allāh opens Makkah to us.» His guide to Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad was Thābit b. Thaʿlabah al-Khazrajī.[^s4.309][^s4.315]

## At Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad

The Prophet ﷺ sent a scouting party from Aslam on the tracks of the enemy: Salīṭ and Nuʿmān, sons of Sufyān b. Ṭalq b. ʿAwf b. Dārim of Banū Sahm, and a third man of Banū ʿUwayr, a clan of Aslam, who is not named. Two of them caught up with Quraysh at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, where the people were making a loud noise as they urged one another to return while Ṣafwān b. Umayyah forbade them. The polytheists saw the two men, turned on them, killed them and went on. The Prophet ﷺ went on with his Companions until he camped at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, and buried the two in one grave; they are *al-Qarīnān*, "the pair".[^s4.310]

ʿAbdullāh b. Sahl and Rāfiʿ b. Sahl of Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal had returned from Uḥud with many wounds, ʿAbdullāh the more severely hurt. When they heard of the Prophet's ﷺ going out, one said to the other that missing an expedition with the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ would be a loss, but that they had no mount. ʿAbdullāh proposed that they drag themselves along and head for him; Rāfiʿ said he had no walking in him, then grew weak, and ʿAbdullāh carried him on his back for a turn while the other walked for a turn. They reached the Prophet ﷺ at the time of *ʿishāʾ* while the fires were being lit. ʿAbbād b. Bishr was on guard that night. The Prophet ﷺ asked what had held them back, and they told him of their ailment; he prayed for good for them and said, «If a span of life is long for you two, you will have mounts of horses, mules and camels — and that is not better for you.» It is said that these two were Anas and Muʾnis, sons of Faḍālah, of Banū Ẓafar.[^s4.310]

Jābir ﵁ said that most of their provisions were dates, and that Saʿd b. ʿUbādah ﵁ carried thirty camels to Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad and drove slaughter-animals, which they slaughtered.[^s4.310] The Prophet ﷺ ordered them by day to gather firewood and at evening to light fires, each man lighting one. They lit five hundred fires, which were seen from a distant place, and word of the Muslims' camp and their fires went in every direction; by this Allāh humbled His enemy.[^s4.310]

Maʿbad b. Abī Maʿbad al-Khuzāʿī, then a polytheist, met the Prophet ﷺ. Khuzāʿah, Muslim and unbeliever alike, were a repository of counsel for him in Tihāmah and hid from him nothing that happened there. Maʿbad told him that what had befallen him and his Companions had been hard on them, and that they would have liked Allāh to raise his standing and the affliction to have fallen on someone other than him.[^s4.310][^s4.311] ʿAmr b. al-Jawzī, in *al-Talqīḥ*, was positive of Maʿbad's Islam.[^s4.310]

## Maʿbad and Abū Sufyān

Maʿbad went on, while the Prophet ﷺ was at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, until he came to Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb and those with him at al-Rawḥāʾ, who had resolved to return to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, saying that they had struck his best Companions, their leaders and nobles, and should return before wiping them out, to charge the remnant and finish with them. Seeing Maʿbad, Abū Sufyān said that he had the news and asked what was behind him. Maʿbad said that he had left Muḥammad and his Companions gone out seeking them with a host the like of which he had never seen, burning against them; that those who had stayed behind from him the day before, of the Aws and Khazraj, had gathered with him and pledged not to return until they overtook Quraysh and took vengeance; that they were intensely angry for their people and regretted what they had done. Abū Sufyān said, "Woe to you! What are you saying!" Maʿbad answered that he did not think they would set out before they saw the forelocks of the horses. Abū Sufyān said they had resolved to charge them and wipe out their remnant; Maʿbad said, "Then I forbid you that," and added that what he had seen had moved him to compose lines of verse about them.[^s4.311]

This, together with Ṣafwān's words, turned Abū Sufyān and those with him back; they went off quickly, fearing pursuit.[^s4.311] A party of riders of ʿAbd al-Qays, bound for Madinah for provisions, passed Abū Sufyān. He asked whether they would carry a message to Muḥammad, promising to load their camels with raisins at ʿUkāẓ when they arrived there, and they agreed. The message was that Quraysh had resolved to march against him and his Companions to wipe out the remnant and were on the riders' tracks. The riders reached the Prophet ﷺ at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad and told him what Abū Sufyān and his companions had said, and he said: «Allāh is sufficient for us, and excellent is the Disposer of affairs.»[^s4.311]

## Muʿāwiyah b. al-Mughīrah and Abū ʿAzzah

On this expedition, before his return to Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ seized Muʿāwiyah b. al-Mughīrah b. Abī al-ʿĀṣ b. Umayyah. He had taken refuge with ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān ﵁, who asked the Prophet ﷺ for safe-conduct for him; it was granted on the condition that, if he were found after three days, he would be killed. He stayed beyond the three days and hid. The Prophet ﷺ sent Zayd b. Ḥārithah and ʿAmmār b. Yāsir ﵄, telling them they would find him in such-and-such a place; they found him and killed him.[^s4.311][^s4.312]

He also seized Abū ʿAzzah al-Jumaḥī, whom the Prophet ﷺ had shown favour at Badr. Abū ʿAzzah said, «O Messenger of Allāh, excuse me.» The Prophet ﷺ said, «By Allāh, you shall not wipe your cheeks in Makkah …»; the report continues, «and say: "I deceived Muḥammad twice." Strike his neck, Zubayr,» and al-Zubayr struck his neck.[^s4.312] The saying that the believer is not stung from a hole twice reached Ibn Hishām from Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab; it is reported by al-Bukhārī and others from Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab from Abū Hurayrah, raised to the Prophet ﷺ.[^s4.312]

## Return and revelation

The Prophet ﷺ went back after staying at Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday; al-Balādhurī says he was absent from Madinah five days.[^s4.312] Allāh revealed: "Those who responded to Allāh and the Messenger" (Āl ʿImrān 172), meaning his call to go out to fight when Abū Sufyān wished to return, "after the wound had befallen them", at Uḥud. "The people said to them" is explained as Nuʿaym b. Masʿūd al-Ashjaʿī, and "the people have gathered hosts against you" as meaning to wipe them out. The saying «Allāh is sufficient for us, and excellent is the Disposer of affairs» (Āl ʿImrān 173): Ibn ʿAbbās reported that Ibrāhīm said it when he was thrown into the fire and Muḥammad said it when they said that the people had gathered against them.[^s4.312][^s4.313] "They returned with favour from Allāh and bounty" is glossed as in safety, "no harm touched them" as of killing or wounding, and "they followed the pleasure of Allāh" as by obeying Him and His Messenger in going out.[^s4.312][^s4.313] They also made an appointment with the Prophet ﷺ for the market of Badr in the year after Uḥud.[^s4.312]

## Disputes

**The cause.** They differed about the cause of the expedition. Ibn Isḥāq and those who follow him said that the Prophet ﷺ went out only to cause fear to the enemy, so that it would reach them that he had gone in pursuit and they would think him strong and not weakened by what had befallen him. Mūsā b. ʿUqbah and al-Wāqidī said that it reached him that Abū Sufyān and most of those with him wanted to return to wipe out the Companions who remained, and that he then urged the people to pursue.[^s4.308] The second is supported by a report from Ibn ʿAbbās, with a sound chain, in al-Firyābī, al-Nasāʾī and al-Ṭabarānī: when the polytheists turned back from Uḥud they said, "You neither killed Muḥammad nor took the *kawāʿib* (young women) up behind you; evil is what you have done — go back." The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ heard of it, called the Muslims, and they responded.[^s4.308][^s4.314]

**The date.** He went out on Sunday morning when sixteen nights of Shawwāl had passed; with Ibn Saʿd, when eight had passed. The disagreement among them is about the date of Uḥud.[^s4.313]

**Jābir's sisters.** His sisters are given as seven, and in one wording nine, "and that is the sound one".[^s4.309]

**Who went out.** ʿĀʾishah ﵂ reported that when the polytheists had gone away he feared they would return and asked who would go on their tracks, and seventy men responded, among them Abū Bakr and al-Zubayr; the two Shaykhs and others report this from ʿUrwah. In al-Ṭabarānī, from Ibn ʿAbbās, the names are Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, ʿAlī, ʿAmmār b. Yāsir, Ṭalḥah, Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ, ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. ʿAwf, Abū Ḥudhayfah and Ibn Masʿūd.[^s4.313] Ibn Kathīr calls this account very strange, since what is well known among the authors of the *maghāzī* is that all who were present at Uḥud went out, seven hundred, of whom seventy had been killed and the rest remained. Al-Ṣāliḥī's own view is that there is no conflict: ʿĀʾishah's "seventy responded" means that they went ahead of the others and the rest caught up, a point he says al-Ḥāfiẓ did not make in *al-Fatḥ*.[^s4.314]

**The occasion of the verses.** They differed about the occasion of revelation of the verse: from Mujāhid and a group, that it was revealed concerning the Prophet's ﷺ going out to Badr al-Mawʿid; others held that it was revealed when he went out to Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad, which al-Bukhārī's arrangement implies and Ibn Jarīr preferred. Ibn Mardawayh and al-Khaṭīb report this from Ibn ʿAbbās, and ʿAbd b. Ḥumayd and Ibn Jarīr from Qatādah and others.[^s4.313]
