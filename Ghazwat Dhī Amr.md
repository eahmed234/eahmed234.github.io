# Ghazwat Dhī Amr (غزوة ذي أمر)
*The expedition of Ghaṭafān to Najd, at which Duʿthūr b. al-Ḥārith drew his sword on the Prophet ﷺ*

**Ghazwat Dhī Amr** is the expedition of the Prophet ﷺ to Dhū Amr in Najd, against a gathering of [[Ghaṭafān]]. It is also called the expedition of Ghaṭafān to Najd.[^s4.176] In the order of Ibn Isḥāq it comes after [[Ghazwat al-Suwayq]] and [[Ghazwat Banī Sulaym]] (at al-Kudr) and before the expedition of al-Furuʿ.[^s4.172]
## Cause and departure
It reached the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ that a gathering of Banū Thaʿlabah b. Saʿīd b. Dhubyān b. Baghīḍ b. Rayth b. Ghaṭafān and of Banū Muḥārib b. Khaṣafah b. Qays had assembled at Dhū Amr, intending to strike at the outskirts of his territory. A man of theirs called [[Duʿthūr b. al-Ḥārith]] b. Muḥārib had gathered them.[^s4.176] The Prophet ﷺ called the Muslims out and set out with four hundred and fifty men, who had a number of horses with them, leaving [[ʿUthmān b. ʿAffān]] ﵁ as deputy over Madinah.[^s4.176]

## Jabbār and the march
The Muslims came upon a man of Banū Thaʿlabah called Jabbār at Dhū al-Qiṣṣah, a valley twenty-four miles from Madinah.[^s4.176][^s4.177] Asked where he was going, he said he wanted Yathrib, to look about for himself. He was brought to the Prophet ﷺ and told him of the enemy: they would not meet the Muslims, and if they heard of the march they would flee to the mountain tops; he would travel with them. The Prophet ﷺ called him to Islam and he became Muslim, and the Prophet ﷺ attached him to [[Bilāl b. Rabāḥ]] ﵁.[^s4.176] Jabbār took a road with him and brought him down upon them; the people had heard of the march and fled to the mountain tops. The Prophet ﷺ reached a water called Dhū Amr and encamped there.[^s4.176]

## Duʿthūr
Heavy rain fell on the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions and soaked their clothes. The Prophet ﷺ alighted beneath a tree, spread out his clothes to dry and lay down, in sight of the polytheists, while the Muslims were busy with their affairs. The polytheists sent Duʿthūr b. al-Ḥārith, their chief and the bravest of them, with a sword slung on him. He came wrapped over the sword until he stood at the Prophet's ﷺ head with the sword drawn, and said, "O Muḥammad, who will protect you from me today?" The Prophet ﷺ said, "Allāh." Jibrīl ﵊ pushed Duʿthūr in the chest and the sword fell from his hand. The Prophet ﷺ took it and said, "Who will protect you from me?" Duʿthūr answered, "No one," and bore witness that there is no god but Allāh and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allāh, swearing never again to increase a gathering against him. The Prophet ﷺ gave him his sword.[^s4.176]

Duʿthūr went back to his people, who asked what was the matter with him. He said that he had looked at a tall man who pushed him in the chest so that he fell on his back, that he knew him to be an angel, and that he bore witness that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allāh. He began calling his people to Islam.[^s4.176] Allāh sent down: "O you who believe, remember the favour of Allāh upon you when a people resolved to stretch out their hands against you, and He withheld their hands from you" ([[Sūrat al-Māʾidah]] 5:11).[^s4.176]

## Return
The Prophet ﷺ returned to Madinah and met no fighting.[^s4.176] His absence was fifteen nights.[^s4.176]

## Disputes
**Length of the stay.** Abū ʿUmar said that the Prophet ﷺ stayed in Najd the whole of Ṣafar.[^s4.176]

**Whether it is the same story as Dhāt al-Riqāʿ.** al-Bayhaqī said that a story resembling that of Duʿthūr will come in [[Ghazwat Dhāt al-Riqāʿ]], and that perhaps they are two stories.[^s4.177] It is said in *al-Bidāyah* that, if this one is preserved, it is certainly a different one: the man in that story was also named Ghawrath [b. al-Ḥārith] and did not become Muslim but stayed on his religion, though he made a covenant with the Prophet ﷺ not to fight him.[^s4.177]
