# Sariyyat ʿAbdillāh b. Jaḥsh ilā Baṭn Nakhlah (سرية عبد الله بن جحش إلى بطن نخلة)
*The expedition of ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh to Baṭn Nakhlah in Rajab of the second year: the letter opened after two days, the first fifth taken in Islam, and the revelation of al-Baqarah 2:217.*[^s6.16][^s6.17][^s6.18]

The Sariyyat ʿAbdillāh b. Jaḥsh was an expedition of the Muhājirūn sent by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in Rajab of the second year to Baṭn Nakhlah.[^s6.16] It was led by [[ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh]] ﵁, called the Commander of the Believers and the one mutilated in Allah's cause,[^s6.16] who was the first commander in Islam.[^s6.16][^i4.31] At Nakhlah his men killed ʿAmr b. al-Ḥaḍramī of a Quraysh caravan and took two captives and the caravan; the first fifth of booty taken in Islam was set aside from it.[^s6.18] The verse al-Baqarah 2:217 was revealed about it.[^s6.18]

## The commission

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ called ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh after the ʿishāʾ prayer and told him to present himself at dawn with his weapons, for he would send him on a mission. ʿAbdullāh came at dawn with his bow, sword, quiver and shield, and after the Prophet ﷺ had led the dawn prayer and turned away he found ʿAbdullāh already standing at his door, with some men of Quraysh.[^s6.16] The Prophet ﷺ called [[Ubayy b. Kaʿb]], who wrote a letter, and gave ʿAbdullāh a sheet of Khawlānī leather, saying: "I have appointed you over these men. Go on, and when you have travelled two nights, look at this letter of mine, then go on to what is in it." Asked which direction, he said: "Take the Najdī route, making for Rakbah."[^s6.16]

With him he sent eight men of the [[al-Muhājirūn]], none of them an Anṣārī: Abū Ḥudhayfah b. ʿUtbah, [[Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ]], ʿUkkāshah b. Miḥṣan, [[ʿUtbah b. Ghazwān]], ʿĀmir b. Rabīʿah, [[Wāqid b. ʿAbdillāh]], Khālid b. al-Bukayr and Suhayl b. Bayḍāʾ.[^s6.16]

## The march and the letter

When ʿAbdullāh had gone a two days' journey he opened the letter. In it was: "Go in the name of Allah and His blessings. Do not compel any of your companions to travel with you, and go on for my command with whoever follows you until you come to Baṭn Nakhlah, and watch for the caravan of Quraysh and find out for us their news." He said: "Hearing and obedience," and read it to his companions, telling them that the Prophet ﷺ had forbidden him to compel any of them: whoever wanted martyrdom and desired it should go, and whoever disliked that should return. They all said they were hearing and obeying Allah, His Messenger ﷺ and him, and told him to go on with Allah's blessing. None stayed behind, and he took the route by the Ḥijāz.[^s6.17]

At a place called Buḥrān, at a mine above al-Furʿ, Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ and ʿUtbah b. Ghazwān lost a camel on which the two took turns. They stayed behind two days searching for it, did not witness the engagement, and reached Madinah some days after the others.[^s6.17][^s6.20] ʿAbdullāh went on with the rest of his companions and alighted at Nakhlah.[^s6.17]

## The encounter at Nakhlah

A caravan of Quraysh passed by, carrying raisins, leather and trade goods that they had brought from al-Ṭāʾif. In it were ʿAmr b. al-Ḥaḍramī, ʿUthmān b. ʿAbdillāh b. al-Mughīrah al-Makhzūmī, his brother Nawfal b. ʿAbdillāh, and al-Ḥakam b. Kaysān, client of Hishām b. al-Mughīrah. The people of the caravan, seeing the Muslims alighted near them, feared them and disapproved of their affair. ʿUkkāshah b. Miḥṣan shaved his head and appeared so that they would be reassured; seeing him they said there was nothing to fear from them, that they were a people of ʿUmmār. They felt safe, hobbled their mounts, let them loose and made food.[^s6.17]

The Muslims consulted one another about them on the last day of Rajab, and were in doubt whether that day belonged to the sacred month. They said that if they left the people that night the people would enter the Ḥaram and be protected from them by it, and that if they killed them they would kill them in the sacred month. They hesitated and shrank from advancing on them; then they encouraged themselves and agreed to kill whomever they could and take what they had.[^s6.17] Wāqid b. ʿAbdillāh al-Tamīmī shot ʿAmr b. al-Ḥaḍramī with an arrow and killed him, and the Muslims charged. They captured ʿUthmān b. ʿAbdillāh and al-Ḥakam b. Kaysān, the latter taken by [[al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr]], and Nawfal b. ʿAbdillāh got away.[^s6.17][^s6.18][^i6.465]

The Muslims took the caravan. ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh set aside a fifth of the booty for the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and divided the rest among his companions. It was the first fifth taken in Islam and the first booty; ʿAmr b. al-Ḥaḍramī was the first killed at the hands of the Muslims, and ʿUthmān b. ʿAbdillāh and al-Ḥakam b. Kaysān were the first captives in Islam.[^s6.18] This was before the fifth of booty was made obligatory; when Allah later made the *fayʾ* lawful, ordered its division and made the fifth obligatory in it, it came out according to what ʿAbdullāh had done with that caravan.[^s6.18] It is reported with a good chain from Zirr b. Ḥubaysh that the first property of which a fifth was taken in Islam was the property of ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh, and that the first banner raised in Islam was his.[^s6.17][^s6.18][^i4.32]

## In Madinah

ʿAbdullāh went with the caravan and the two captives to Madinah. When he came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, the Prophet said: "I did not order you to fight in the sacred month." He held the caravan and the two captives and refused to take any of it. The people were remorseful and thought they had perished, and their brothers among the Muslims rebuked them for what they had done.[^s6.18]

Quraysh said that Muḥammad and his companions had made the sacred month lawful, shed blood in it, taken property in it and taken men captive in it. The Jews, taking an omen from the event against the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, said of ʿAmr b. al-Ḥaḍramī, killed by Wāqid b. ʿAbdillāh: "ʿAmr — the war has been kindled; al-Ḥaḍramī — the war has come; Wāqid — the war has been ignited." Allah made that against them, not for them.[^s6.18]

When the people talked much about it, Allah sent down: "They ask you about the sacred month, fighting in it. Say: fighting in it is a great thing, but turning away from the path of Allah, disbelief in Him and the Sacred Mosque, and expelling its people from it are greater with Allah; and *fitnah* is greater than killing" ([[Sūrat al-Baqarah]] 2:217). That is: if they had killed in the sacred month, their enemies had turned them away from the path of Allah, together with disbelief in Him, and from the Sacred Mosque, and had expelled them from it when they were its people, which is greater with Allah than the killing of those they had killed; and Quraysh used to put the Muslim to trial in his religion until they turned him back to disbelief after his faith, which is greater than killing.[^s6.18] When the Qurʾān came down about this and Allah relieved the Muslims of their apprehension, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ took the booty, or its fifth, and the two captives.[^s6.18]

## The captives and the verse of hope

Quraysh sent to him about the ransom of the two captives. He said: "We shall not ransom them to you until our two companions arrive — meaning Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ and ʿUtbah b. Ghazwān — for we fear you for them; if you kill them we shall kill your two companions." When Saʿd and ʿUtbah arrived, he ransomed the two captives for forty ūqiyyah each.[^s6.19] Al-Ḥakam b. Kaysān became Muslim, his Islam was good, and he stayed with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ until he was killed a martyr on the day of Biʾr Maʿūnah ([[Maʾsāt Biʾr Maʿūnah]]); ʿUthmān b. ʿAbdillāh rejoined Makkah and died an unbeliever.[^s6.19][^i2.95]

When what ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh and his companions were in had cleared away, they hoped for reward and asked whether they might hope that this be an expedition for which they were given the reward of the *mujāhidīn*. Allah sent down: "Those who believed, and those who emigrated and strove in the path of Allah — those hope for the mercy of Allah, and Allah is forgiving, merciful" (al-Baqarah 2:218), and so placed them upon the greatest hope.[^s6.19]

On this expedition ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh was named Commander of the Believers.[^s6.19]

## Disputes and variant reports

The party is said by Ibn ʿĀʾidh to have included Sahl b. Bayḍāʾ, with no mention of Suhayl, Khālid or ʿUkkāshah. Ibn Saʿd lists al-Miqdād b. ʿAmr among them, and says they were twelve, every two taking turns on a camel.[^s6.17] Whether Nawfal b. ʿAbdillāh was in the caravan is disputed: those who say he was say he got away; those who say he was not make the fugitive al-Mughīrah.[^s6.17][^s6.18] It is said that Wāqid b. ʿAbdillāh, rather than ʿUkkāshah, shaved his head.[^s6.17] The day of the consultation is given as the last day of Rajab; it is said the first day of Shaʿbān, and it is said the last day of Jumādā al-Ākhirah.[^s6.17]

Some of the Muslims said they brought the booty in full, rather than setting aside a fifth.[^s6.18] It is said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ held the booty of the people of Nakhlah until he returned from Badr, then divided it with the booty of the people of Badr, giving each group its due; and the report of what he took after the revelation reads "the booty, or its fifth".[^s6.18] Abū Nuʿaym is positive that ʿAbdullāh b. Jaḥsh was the first commander the Messenger of Allah ﷺ appointed, and the report of Saʿd b. Abī Waqqāṣ supports this.[^s6.19]
