# Dustūr al-Madīnah (موادعة النبي ﷺ لليهود وكتابه بين المهاجرين والأنصار)
*The covenant the Prophet ﷺ wrote between the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār, and the truce he made in it with the Jews*

The Prophet ﷺ wrote a document between the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār in which he made a truce and covenant with the Jews, confirmed them in their religion and their property, and laid conditions on them and conditions for them.[^s3.382] It was made when the Jews refused to follow him, and before the permission to fight and to take the *jizyah* from those who refused Islam.[^s3.382] The year is not given.

## The text

Ibn Isḥāq gave the text of the document, about two leaves long, without a chain of transmission; Abū ʿUbayd related it in *Kitāb al-Amwāl* with a good chain from al-Zuhrī, who said it had reached him that the Prophet ﷺ wrote it.[^s3.382][^s3.382n1] What follows is from Abū ʿUbayd's version, as quoted in the editors' note.

**The community.** The document is from Muḥammad the Prophet, the Messenger of Allah, between the believers and Muslims of Quraysh and the people of Yathrib and those who follow them, join them and strive with them; they are «أمة واحدة دون الناس», one community to the exclusion of other people.[^s3.382n1] The believers are allies of one another to the exclusion of others, and they have a single peace: no believer makes peace apart from another in fighting in the way of Allah except on equal and just terms, and each raiding party relieves another by turns.[^s3.382n1]

**Blood-money and ransom.** The Muhājirūn of Quraysh pay blood-money among themselves according to their former practice and ransom their captives with kindness and fairness among the believers.[^s3.382n1] The same is said in turn of Banū ʿAwf, Banū al-Ḥārith b. al-Khazraj, Banū Sāʿidah, Banū Jusham, Banū al-Najjār, Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf, Banū al-Nabīt and Banū al-Aws, each according to its *rabāʿah* (glossed as the *maʿāqil*).[^s3.382n1][^s3.383] The believers do not leave a debtor-burdened man (*mufraḥ*) among them without helping him with kindness, in ransom or in blood-money.[^s3.382n1] Abū ʿUbayd explains the *mufraḥ* as one weighed down by debt: a captive among them is to be freed from captivity, and if he has committed an offence by error, they pay its blood-money for him.[^s3.383]

**Against wrongdoing.** The God-fearing believers' hands are against anyone of them who acts wrongfully or seeks a share of injustice, sin, aggression or corruption among the believers, even if he is the son of one of them. A believer is not to kill a believer on account of an unbeliever, nor to help an unbeliever against a believer. Whoever kills a believer without cause is subject to retaliation unless the slain man's guardian is satisfied with blood-money.[^s3.382n1] Abū ʿUbayd draws from this that the choice between retaliation and blood-money lies with the guardians of the slain.[^s3.383] It is unlawful for a believer who has accepted what is in the document, and who believes in Allah and the Last Day, to help or shelter an offender (*muḥdith*); whoever does so has the curse and wrath of Allah upon him until the Day of Resurrection, and no *ṣarf* or *ʿadl* is accepted from him.[^s3.382n1] Abū ʿUbayd glosses the *muḥdith* as anyone who has committed one of Allah's *ḥudūd*, whom no one may prevent from having the *ḥadd* applied, and quotes Makḥūl that *ṣarf* is repentance and *ʿadl* is ransom.[^s3.383]

**The Jews.** Whoever of the Jews follows the believers has kindness and equal standing, neither wronged nor having others aided against them.[^s3.382n1] The Jews of Banū ʿAwf, their allies and themselves are a community with the believers:

> «لليهود دينهم، وللمؤمنين دينهم»

except whoever does wrong and sins, who ruins only himself and his household. The Jews of Banū al-Najjār, Banū al-Ḥārith, Banū Jusham, Banū Sāʿidah and al-Aws have the same as the Jews of Banū ʿAwf, and the Jews of al-Aws and their allies are with the righteous among the people of the document.[^s3.382n1][^s3.383] None of them is to go out except with Muḥammad's permission.[^s3.382n1] The Jews spend with the believers as long as they are at war;[^s3.382n1] Abū ʿUbayd takes this expenditure to be for war specifically, a condition laid on them to help against his enemy, and thinks that the Jews were given a share when they fought alongside the Muslims only because of this condition.[^s3.383]

**Quraysh.** No polytheist is to give protection to the property of Quraysh or help it against a believer.[^s3.382n1] Abū ʿUbayd takes the polytheist here to mean the Jews with whom the truce was made: it is not part of their truce that they protect the property of his enemies or help them against him.[^s3.383]

**Defence and the sanctuary.** Between the parties there is mutual help against whoever fights the people of the document, and against whoever attacks Yathrib, and sincere counsel and the helping of the wronged. The interior of Madīnah is sacred (*ḥaram*) for the people of the document.[^s3.382n1][^s3.383] If they call the Jews to a peace with an ally of theirs, they make peace with him; and if they call the Muslims to the like, it holds for them upon the believers, except against whoever fights the religion; and each people bears its share of the expenditure.[^s3.383]

**Referral.** Whatever the parties differ about, its judgment is to Allah and to the Messenger ﷺ; and whatever event arises among the people of the document whose corruption is feared, its matter is for Allah and for Muḥammad the Prophet.[^s3.382n1][^s3.383]

**Safety.** Whoever goes out is safe and whoever stays is safe, except whoever does wrong and sins; righteousness is distinct from sin, and no one earns except against himself.[^s3.383]

## The Jewish leaders

The Prophet ﷺ was visited by Abū Yāsir b. Akhṭab, brother of Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab, the first of the Jews to come to him. He listened to him and, returning, told his people: «أطيعوني فإن هذا هو النبي الذي كنا ننتظره» — "Obey me, for this is the Prophet whom we were awaiting." His brother disobeyed him, and the Devil gained mastery over them, so that they obeyed the brother.[^s3.383]

Maymūn b. Yāmīn, the head of the Jews, came to the Prophet ﷺ and asked to be made an arbiter among them, since they deferred to him. The Prophet ﷺ had him go inside and sent for them; when they came he asked them to choose a man as arbiter between himself and them. They said they were content with Maymūn b. Yāmīn, but when he came out and bore witness that the Prophet ﷺ was the Messenger of Allah, they refused to believe him.[^s3.383]

The Prophet ﷺ said that if ten of the rabbis of the Jews believed in him, every Jew on the face of the earth would believe in him.[^s3.383] Among the leaders of the Jews were:

- of Banū Qaynuqāʿ, [[ʿAbdullāh b. Salām]] ﵁, who was well known for leadership among them, with Saʿd b. Ḥunayf, Finḥāṣ and Rifāʿah b. Zayd b. al-Tābūt;[^s3.384]
- of Banū al-Naḍīr, Abū Yāsir b. Akhṭab and his brother Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab, Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf and Abū Rāfiʿ Salām b. al-Rabīʿ b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq;[^s3.384]
- of Banū Qurayẓah, al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā b. Wahb, Kaʿb b. Asad — the holder of the covenant of Banū Qurayẓah, which he broke in the year of al-Aḥzāb — and Shamwīl b. Zayd.[^s3.384]

## Enmity and the breach of the covenant

After this the rabbis of the Jews set up enmity against the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, out of insolence, envy and rancour at God's having singled out the Arabs by choosing His Messenger from among them. They were the ones who questioned him, tried to trip him and brought him confusion to confuse truth with falsehood, and the Qurʾān was revealed about them and what they asked, apart from a few questions on the lawful and the forbidden that the Muslims asked.[^s3.384]

The Jews were three tribes: Qaynuqāʿ, Qurayẓah and al-Naḍīr. All three fought him and broke the covenant between him and them. He showed favour to [[Banū Qaynuqāʿ]], expelled [[Banū al-Naḍīr]], and killed [[Banū Qurayẓah]] and took their offspring captive. [[Sūrat al-Ḥashr]] was revealed about Banū al-Naḍīr and [[Sūrat al-Aḥzāb]] about Banū Qurayẓah.[^s3.384] See [[Ghazwat Banī Qaynuqāʿ]], [[Ghazwat Banī al-Naḍīr]] and [[Ghazwat Banī Qurayẓah]].

## Disputes

**How many believers were needed.** The report that ten rabbis would have sufficed is given in another transmission with the addition "Kaʿb said: twelve", which is confirmed by the twelve chiefs of [[Sūrat al-Māʾidah]] (5:12). On this, Ibn Ḥajar says, ten particular persons are meant, since otherwise more than ten had believed in him.[^s3.383] On who they were, he says it appears they were the chiefs of the Jews at that time, with the rest following them, so that few became Muslim, such as ʿAbdullāh b. Salām; each of the leaders listed above was a chief whose conversion a group would have followed, so it is possible that they are the ones meant.[^s3.384] In another report from Abū Hurayrah the words are that if al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā and those of his rank among the chiefs believed in him, they would all become Muslim.[^s3.384]

**Al-Suhaylī's count.** al-Suhaylī held, against this, that of the rabbis of the Jews only two became Muslim, ʿAbdullāh b. Salām and ʿAbdullāh b. Ṣūrī; this is described as a strange statement, and Ibn Ḥajar says he has seen no sound route for ʿAbdullāh b. Ṣūrī's Islam, which al-Suhaylī elsewhere takes from al-Naqqāsh's *tafsīr*.[^s3.384]
