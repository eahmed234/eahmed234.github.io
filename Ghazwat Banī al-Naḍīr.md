# Ghazwat Banī al-Naḍīr (غزوة بني النضير)
*The expedition against the Jewish clan of Banū al-Naḍīr, their expulsion from Madinah, and the revelation of Sūrat al-Ḥashr*[^s4.317]

The expedition against Banū al-Naḍīr ended with the departure of the clan from Madinah. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ besieged their fortresses, ordered their palms cut, and accepted their surrender on the terms that they leave with what their camels could carry, except arms.[^s4.323][^s4.324] He took their property and arms and divided it among the Muhājirūn.[^s4.324][^s4.325] Most of Sūrat al-Ḥashr was revealed concerning them.[^s4.327]

## Cause

The cause is disputed. One account, reported from a man of the Companions through ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Kaʿb b. Mālik, begins before Badr, when the unbelievers of Quraysh wrote to ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy and those of the Aws and Khazraj who worshipped idols, swearing that they would fight him or expel him, or else call the Arabs against them and march on them all together. When this reached Ibn Ubayy and his people they gathered to fight the Messenger of Allah ﷺ; he met them with a group of his Companions and told them they were intending to fight their own sons and brothers, and they dispersed. After Badr, Quraysh wrote to the Jews that they were the people of arms and fortresses and must fight "our companion". When the letter reached them, Banū al-Naḍīr gathered in treachery and sent word that he should come out with thirty of his Companions and meet thirty of their rabbis at a place midway, to hear him and believe if the rabbis did. He went out the next day with thirty men and thirty rabbis came out; in the open ground some of the Jews said that thirty Companions, each wishing to die before him, made it impossible to get at him, and they asked him to come with three Companions and meet three of their scholars. He went out with three, and the three Jews concealed daggers, intending to kill him by surprise. A woman of Banū al-Naḍīr who wished him well sent word to her brother, a Muslim of the Anṣār, who caught up with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and told him privately before he reached them, and he returned to Madinah.[^s4.317][^s4.317n1]

The other account, given by Ibn Isḥāq, Ibn ʿUmar, Ibn Saʿd, Ibn ʿĀʾidh and most of the people of the *maghāzī*, begins with ʿAmr b. Umayyah al-Ḍamrī ﵁. Coming from Biʾr Maʿūnah, he met at Qanāh two men of Banū ʿĀmir b. Ṣaʿṣaʿah with whom the Prophet ﷺ had made a truce; he asked their lineage, took the midday rest with them, and when they slept he leapt on them and killed them. He told the Prophet ﷺ, who said: "Evil is what you did — they had from us security." ʿAmr replied that he had not known, had thought them still polytheists, and that their people had treated the Muslims treacherously. The Prophet ﷺ ordered the men's spoils set aside to be sent with their blood-money. Banū al-Naḍīr and Banū ʿĀmir had a contract and alliance (*ḥilf*), so on a Saturday he went, prayed in Masjid Qubāʾ with a group of the Muhājirūn and Anṣār, and came to Banū al-Naḍīr with fewer than ten Companions, to ask their help with the blood-money of the two Kilābīs.[^s4.318]

## The plot at the wall

Banū al-Naḍīr said they would do as he wished and asked him to sit and eat while they consulted; he was leaning against one of their houses. Withdrawing privately, Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab said that Muḥammad had come with a few Companions, not ten — Abū Bakr, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, ʿAlī, al-Zubayr, Ṭalḥah, Saʿd b. Muʿādh, Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr and Saʿd b. ʿUbādah — and urged them to throw stones on him from above the house and kill him, since they would never find him more alone. ʿAmr b. Jaḥḥāsh al-Naḍrī said he would throw a rock when the Prophet ﷺ was beneath. Sallām b. Mishkam told them to obey him this once: the Prophet ﷺ would be informed, and it would break the covenant between them. ʿAmr b. Jaḥḥāsh prepared the rock; as he appeared above with it, news came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ from heaven of what they intended. He rose quickly as though for some need and headed toward Madinah, while his Companions sat talking, thinking he had gone to relieve himself.[^s4.318]

According to ʿIkrimah, a Jew arrived from Madinah while the Jews were in this state, asked what they wanted, and was told they wanted to kill Muḥammad and seize his Companions; he said he had left Muḥammad inside Madinah, and they were dismayed.[^s4.318] The Companions grew impatient at his absence and, concluding that he had gone off on some matter, got up to look for him. Ḥuyayy said that Abū al-Qāsim had been hasty, and the Jews regretted what they had done.[^s4.318][^s4.319]

Kinānah b. Ṣuwayrāʾ then asked them whether they knew why Muḥammad had risen; they said they did not, and he said that Muḥammad had been told what they intended of treachery. He swore by the Torah that Muḥammad was the Messenger of Allah and the last of the prophets, and that their books said his birth was in Makkah, the abode of his hijrah Yathrib, and his description exactly as in them. He urged two things on them: to become Muslims, securing their wealth and children and remaining in their homes; failing that, to say yes when he sent to them to leave his land, since he would not take their blood or wealth and they might sell or keep it. They answered that they would not leave the Torah and the covenant of Mūsā; Sallām b. Mishkam told Ḥuyayy to agree to leave.[^s4.319]

His Companions met a man coming out of Madinah who had met the Prophet ﷺ at the bridge going in; they found he had sent for Muḥammad b. Maslamah. Abū Bakr said: "You got up and we did not notice." The Messenger of Allah ﷺ replied: "The Jews intended treachery against me; Allah informed me, so I got up." Concerning this, it is reported, Allah revealed His words in [[Sūrat al-Māʾidah]] (5:11), "O you who believe, remember the favour of Allah upon you when a people intended to stretch out their hands against you."[^s4.319]

## Muḥammad b. Maslamah's message

The Prophet ﷺ told [[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]] to go to the Jews of Banū al-Naḍīr and tell them that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had sent him to say: leave my land. Before delivering it he adjured them by the Torah to confirm that he had come to them before Muḥammad was sent and that, when they offered to feed him or make him a Jew, he had said to feed him and never to make him a Jew; they had said that nothing kept him from their religion except that it was the Jews' religion, and described to him the one who would come, "the one who laughs often and fights", with redness in his eyes, coming from Yemen, riding the camel, wearing the *shamlah*, content with a crust and his sword on his shoulder. They answered that they had said it, but that it was not he.[^s4.319][^s4.320]

He then delivered the message: they had broken the covenant the Prophet ﷺ made with them by what they intended of treachery against him. He told them what they had planned, including ʿAmr b. Jaḥḥāsh's appearing on the house to throw the rock, and they fell silent. He said: "Leave my land; I have given you ten days' respite; whoever is seen after that, his neck will be struck." They said they had not thought a man of the Aws would bring this; he said: "Hearts have changed." They stayed several days preparing, sent for their animals at Dhū al-Judur and hired camels from men of Ashjaʿ.[^s4.320]

## Ibn Ubayy's promise

While they were preparing, two messengers of [[ʿAbdullāh b. Ubayy b. Salūl]], Suwayd and Dāʿis, came to them: they were not to leave their homes and wealth but to stay in their fortresses, for he had two thousand of his people and other Arabs who would enter with them and die to the last man before they were reached; Qurayẓah and their allies of Ghaṭafān would support them. Ibn Ubayy asked Kaʿb b. Asad of Qurayẓah to support them, and Kaʿb said that not one man of them would break the covenant.[^s4.320] Ḥuyayy, lured by Ibn Ubayy's word, decided to tell the Prophet ﷺ that they would not leave their land and wealth. Sallām b. Mishkam rebuked him: Ibn Ubayy only wanted to push him into destruction and then sit in his house, as he had done with his allies of Banū Qaynuqāʿ, who fought, shut themselves in their fortresses and surrendered to Muḥammad's judgement. Ḥuyayy refused anything but fighting him.[^s4.320][^s4.321]

Ḥuyayy sent his brother Judayy b. Akhṭab to tell the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: "We shall not leave our homes and wealth; do what you will do," and to go on to Ibn Ubayy and bid him hasten the help he had promised. When Judayy told the Prophet ﷺ, sitting among his Companions, he raised the takbīr aloud, the Muslims made takbīr with him, and he said: "The Jews have made war." Judayy found Ibn Ubayy sitting in his house with some allies, and Ibn Ubayy's son ʿAbdullāh entered in his mail with his sword; seeing Ibn Ubayy in a corner of the house and his son armed, Judayy despaired of help and returned to Ḥuyayy, who was told that Ibn Ubayy had said he would send to his allies of Ghaṭafān to enter with them.[^s4.321][^s4.322]

## The siege

The Prophet ﷺ marched with his Companions to Banū al-Naḍīr, leaving [[ʿAbdullāh b. Umm Maktūm]] as deputy over Madinah. A dome of *gharb* wood covered with haircloth, which Saʿd b. ʿUbādah ﵁ had sent, was carried with him. He prayed ʿAṣr in the open space of Banū al-Naḍīr; when they saw him and his Companions they stood on the walls of their fortresses with arrows and stones. Banū Qurayẓah withdrew from them and did not help them with arms or men.[^s4.322]

After ʿIshāʾ the Prophet ﷺ returned to his house with ten Companions, in mail, on a horse, and put ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib over the army; it is said that it was Abū Bakr. The Muslims spent the night besieging them and making takbīr until morning, when Bilāl called the adhān. The Prophet ﷺ led the people in prayer in the open space of Banū Khaṭmah and had the dome pitched at the place of the small mosque there. A left-handed archer of the Jews called ʿAzzūk shot arrows that reached the dome, and he had it moved to Masjid al-Faḍīkh, farther from the arrows. By evening Ibn Ubayy and his allies had not come near, and Banū al-Naḍīr despaired of his help; Sallām b. Mishkam and Kinānah b. Ṣuwayrāʾ kept asking Ḥuyayy where Ibn Ubayy's help was, and he said it was a *malḥamah* (slaughter) written against them.[^s4.322]

As the siege continued, ʿAlī ﵁ was missing one night near ʿIshāʾ. The Prophet ﷺ told the people to leave him, for he was on some business of theirs. After a little he came with the head of ʿAzzūk, having lain in ambush for him when he went out seeking an unguarded moment against the Muslims; he had charged and killed him, and those with ʿAzzūk fled. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ sent Abū Dujānah and Sahl b. Ḥunayf ﵄ with ten Companions, who overtook the Jews who had fled from ʿAlī and killed them, and their heads were thrown into one of the wells. Saʿd b. ʿUbādah ﵁ carried dates to the Muslims.[^s4.322][^s4.323]

### The palms

Ibn ʿUmar reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ burned the palms of Banū al-Naḍīr and cut them — it is al-Buwayrah — and that Allah revealed: "What you cut of *līnah* or left standing on its roots, it was by Allah's leave."[^bukh4031][^musl1746][^bukh3021] He appointed Abū Laylā al-Māzinī and ʿAbdullāh b. Salām over the cutting. Abū Laylā cut the *ʿajwah*, saying it was more burning for them, and ʿAbdullāh b. Salām cut the *lūn*, saying he knew that Allah would give the Prophet ﷺ their wealth as booty. The *ʿajwah* was the best of their palms; when it was cut the women tore their garment-fronts, struck their cheeks and cried for woe. Sallām b. Mishkam asked Ḥuyayy whether a cluster-palm that does not bear for thirty years should be cut. Ḥuyayy sent to the Prophet ﷺ that he used to forbid corruption, so why was he cutting the palms? Some Muslims felt uneasy at this and feared it might be corruption; some said not to cut, others said to cut in order to enrage them.[^s4.323]

## Surrender and terms

Ḥuyayy sent to the Prophet ﷺ that they would give what he had asked and leave his land. The Prophet ﷺ replied that he would not accept it that day, but they were to leave and have what the camels carried, except the arms (*ḥalqah*). Sallām b. Mishkam told Ḥuyayy to accept before something worse was demanded — the capture of their offspring and the killing of their fighters along with the wealth — but Ḥuyayy refused for a day or two.[^s4.323]

Seeing this, Yāmīn b. ʿUmayr and Abū Saʿd b. Wahb agreed that Muḥammad was the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and wondered what they were waiting for; they came down in the night, became Muslims and secured their wealth and blood. Banū al-Naḍīr then came down on the terms that they have what the camels carry, except the arms.[^s4.323] Yāmīn offered a man of Qays ten dinars, or, it is said, five *wasqs* of dates, until he killed ʿAmr b. Jaḥḥāsh by stealth; the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was pleased at his killing.[^s4.323]

The length of the siege is disputed. Muḥammad b. ʿUmar, Ibn Saʿd, al-Balādhurī, Abū Maʿshar and Ibn Ḥibbān say fifteen days; Ibn Isḥāq and Abū ʿAmr say six nights; Sulaymān al-Taymī says close to twenty nights; Ibn al-Kalāʿ twenty-three nights; and from ʿĀʾishah it is twenty-five, until he expelled them.[^s4.323][^s4.324]

## The departure

[[Muḥammad b. Maslamah]] ﵁ took charge of their expulsion. They said they had debts owed by people, due at fixed terms, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "Take early payment and discount." Abū Rāfiʿ Sallām b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq had a hundred and twenty dinars owed by [[Usayd b. Ḥuḍayr]] due in a year, and settled for his capital, eighty dinars, cancelling the rest. During the siege they had demolished their houses from their side, and the Muslims demolished and burned from theirs, until the settlement; on leaving, a man would pull down his house even to the lintel of his door.[^s4.324]

They went out by Balḥārith b. al-Khazraj, then al-Jubliyyah, then the bridge, passed the *muṣallā* and cut through the market of Madinah, the women in litters wearing brocade, silk, green and red plush and ornaments of gold and silver, with tambourines, pipes and singing-girls playing behind them as a show of fortitude, while the people lined up to watch. They went in strings on six hundred camels, and the hypocrites grieved intensely at their departure. Abū Rāfiʿ Sallām b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq called out that if they had left the palms they would come to palms at Khaybar. Most of them settled at Khaybar, among them Ḥuyayy b. Akhṭab, Sallām b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq and Kinānah b. Ṣuwayrāʾ, and its people submitted to them; a group went to al-Shām.[^s4.324]

## The property

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ took the wealth and the arms, and found fifty coats of mail, fifty helmets and three hundred and forty swords. ʿUmar asked whether he would not take a fifth of what he had got, and he said he would not make into something for the believers what Allah had made his alone, citing al-Ḥashr 7. Banū al-Naḍīr's property was among his choice portions (*ṣafāyā*); he made it an endowment (*ḥabs*) for his contingencies and spent on his family from it. He sowed under the palms and set aside from it a year's food of barley and dates for his wives and Banū ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, and put what remained into horses and weapons.[^s4.324]

When the Prophet ﷺ had moved from Banū ʿAmr b. ʿAwf to Madinah, the Muhājirūn moved too, and the Anṣār vied for them; none lodged with an Anṣārī except by lot, so the Muhājirūn were in the houses and wealth of the Anṣār. On taking Banū al-Naḍīr he had [[Thābit b. Qays]] call the Anṣār, all of them, and spoke: he praised Allah, mentioned what the Anṣār had done for the Muhājirūn, and said that if they wished he would divide the *fayʾ* between them and the Muhājirūn, the Muhājirūn remaining in their homes and wealth, or give it to the Muhājirūn alone and they would leave the Anṣār's houses. [[Saʿd b. ʿUbādah]] and [[Saʿd b. Muʿādh]] ﵄ said that he should rather divide it among the Muhājirūn, who would remain in their houses as before; the Anṣār called out that they were content and submitted. He said: "O Allah, have mercy on the Anṣār and the sons of the Anṣār." He divided the *fayʾ* and gave the Muhājirūn, and gave none of the Anṣār anything of it except two needy men, Sahl b. Ḥunayf and Abū Dujānah; to Saʿd b. Muʿādh he gave the sword of Ibn Abī al-Ḥuqayq, a sword of repute among them.[^s4.325]

In a report given from al-Balādhurī's *Futūḥ al-Buldān*, he told the Anṣār that their brothers of the Muhājirūn had no wealth, and that they could divide this and their own wealth between all, or keep their wealth while he divided this among the Muhājirūn alone; they chose that he divide it among them and divide for them from their own wealth what he wished, and there was revealed: "…and they prefer others over themselves even though poverty be their lot" (al-Ḥashr 9).[^s4.325] Abū Bakr ﵁ said: "May Allah reward you well, company of the Anṣār."[^s4.325]

## ʿAmr b. Saʿdā and Banū Qurayẓah

When Banū al-Naḍīr had left, ʿAmr b. Saʿdā went round their dwellings, saw ruin, and returned to Banū Qurayẓah, whom he found in the synagogue at prayer. He told them he had seen the homes of their brothers empty, their wealth left and owned by others, and that Muḥammad had struck Ibn al-Ashraf by night in his house, struck Ibn Sunaynah, and struck Banū Qaynuqāʿ and expelled them. He urged them to follow Muḥammad, whom their scholars had described, naming Ibn al-Hayyabān Abū ʿUmayr and Ibn Jawwās, who had come from Bayt al-Maqdis awaiting his coming and told them to follow him. The people fell silent. Al-Zubayr b. Bāṭā said he had read Muḥammad's description in the Torah sent down on Mūsā, and that what kept him from following was Kaʿb b. Asad, the holder of their covenant. Kaʿb said they would stay on their covenant and watch what Ḥuyayy did, and that he could not be content to follow this Israelite. ʿAmr b. Saʿdā said they would need help when Muḥammad marched on them and they hid in fortresses, until they surrendered to his judgement and he struck their necks. While they were so, the Prophet's ﷺ vanguard alighted in their courtyard, and ʿAmr said: "This is what I told you." That was because they broke the covenant of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and fought him at the battle of al-Khandaq.[^s4.326][^s4.327]

## Sūrat al-Ḥashr

Most of Sūrat al-Ḥashr was revealed concerning Banū al-Naḍīr. Saʿīd b. Jubayr said to Ibn ʿAbbās: "Sūrat al-Ḥashr"; he answered that it was revealed concerning Banū al-Naḍīr, and said to call it rather Sūrat al-Naḍīr.[^bukh4882][^s4.327] The verse "He it is who drove out those who disbelieved of the People of the Book" refers to Banū al-Naḍīr, and "from their homes" to their dwellings in Madinah. Their fortresses did not protect them, and Allah's command came to them from where they did not reckon; terror was cast into their hearts, for their master Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf had been killed. They destroyed their houses to carry off the wood and other things they found good in them. Had Allah not decreed exile for them, He would have punished them in this world with killing and captivity, as He did with Qurayẓah. The permission to cut a *līnah* (a palm) or leave it standing was a choice given to the Muslims, and the permission to cut disgraced the transgressors, the Jews, in their objection that cutting fruit trees is corruption.[^s4.327][^s4.328] The *fayʾ* that Allah returned to His Messenger from them was won without the Muslims spurring horses or camels or enduring hardship, so they had no right in it; the Prophet ﷺ was singled out for it and did with it as he willed, giving from it to the Muhājirūn and to three of the Anṣār for their poverty.[^s4.328] The verses on the *fayʾ* of the towns, such as al-Ṣafrāʾ, Wādī al-Qurā and Yanbuʿ, on the kinsmen, the orphans, the poor and the wayfarer, and on the Muhājirūn, the Anṣār who preferred them over themselves, and those who came after them, follow.[^s4.328][^s4.329] The hypocrites who told their brothers of the People of the Book — Banū al-Naḍīr — that they would go out with them if they were driven out, and help them if fought, are called liars: if the Jews are driven out they will not go out with them, and if fought they will not help them.[^s4.329]

## Disputes

**The date.** Ibn al-Qayyim, in *al-Hadī*, reports that al-Zuhrī claimed the expedition took place six months after Badr, and judges this a misapprehension and error: what is beyond doubt is that it took place after Uḥud. Al-Zuhrī transmitted it from ʿUrwah; al-Ḥākim related it and graded it sound, al-Dhahabī approved, and al-Bayhaqī related it from ʿĀʾishah, but al-Bayhaqī said that the mention of ʿĀʾishah is not preserved (*ghayr maḥfūẓ*).[^s4.330][^s4.331]

**The verses on al-Buwayrah.** Ibn ʿUmar said that Ḥassān b. Thābit composed verses about the burning of al-Buwayrah, which Abū Sufyān b. al-Ḥārith, before his Islām, answered. Al-Ḥāfiẓ says that this ascription is the well-known one, as in the Ṣaḥīḥ. Abū al-Fatḥ transmitted from Abū ʿAmr al-Shaybānī that the verse beginning «وهان على سراة بني لؤي» was Abū Sufyān's, and its answer Ḥassān's, and held this more likely than the narration in al-Bukhārī; al-Ḥāfiẓ judges that the Ṣaḥīḥ is sounder, since Quraysh used to support the Prophet's enemies and Ḥassān rebuked them for forsaking Banū al-Naḍīr, and since the land of Banū al-Naḍīr borders that of the Anṣār, so its ruin harms them, unlike the land of Quraysh. Ibn Isḥāq says Ḥassān said it at the expedition of Banū Qurayẓah, mentioning Banū al-Naḍīr in passing. To those who thought it unlikely that Abū Sufyān would pray for the burning of the land of unbelievers like himself, the answer is that religious enmity stood between the People of the Book and idol-worshippers, and that by "its quarters" he meant Madinah, so the prayer returns on the Muslims also.[^s4.331][^s4.332][^bukh3021]

**The "first gathering".** On "at the first gathering" (59:2), it is said that this is their gathering to al-Shām, and that its last is that ʿUmar, in his caliphate, expelled them to Khaybar.[^s4.327]

**The number of Anṣār given from the *fayʾ*.** Two needy Anṣārīs received a share according to one passage; another passage says three.[^s4.325][^s4.328]
