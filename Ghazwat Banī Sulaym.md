# Ghazwat Banī Sulaym (غزوة بني سليم بالكدر)
*The expedition of Banī Sulaym at al-Kudr, also called Qarqarat al-Kudr*

The **Ghazwat Banī Sulaym** was an expedition of the Prophet ﷺ to al-Kudr, after word reached him that a gathering of [[Banū Sulaym|Sulaym]] and [[Ghaṭafān]] was at that place. It is also called Qarqarat al-Kudr.[^s4.172]

## The march to al-Kudr
The Prophet ﷺ was told that a gathering (*jamʿ*) of Sulaym and Ghaṭafān was at this place. He appointed Sibāʿ b. ʿUrfuṭah al-Ghifārī, or Ibn Umm Maktūm, as his deputy over Madinah, and [[ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭālib]] ﵁ carried his white banner. He set out and reached the refuge of their waters, called al-Kudr, but found no one in the encampments.[^s4.172]

## Yasār and the camels
He sent a party of his Companions along the upper part of the valley, and the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ met them in the floor of the valley. There he found herdsmen, among them a youth called Yasār, and asked him about the people. Yasār said he had no knowledge of them: he brought the camels to water only on the *khams* rotation (they come to water, graze three days, and come to water on the fifth), this was a day of the *rubʿ* rotation (they come to water one day, are left two days unwatered, then come to water on the fourth), the people had gone up to the waters, and he and the others were *ʿuzzāb* with the camels.[^s4.172][^s4.173]

The Prophet ﷺ stayed three nights, having secured the camels, and then went down toward Madinah.[^s4.172]

## The division at Ṣirār
They divided the spoils at Ṣirār, three miles from Madinah. The camels were five hundred. He took out the fifth and divided the four-fifths among the Muslims, who were two hundred men; each received two *bakrān*. Yasār fell into the Prophet's ﷺ share, and he freed him because he saw him praying.[^s4.172] Ṣirār is an ancient well; it is also said to be a place three miles from Madinah on the road to Iraq.[^s4.173]

## After the return
The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was absent fifteen nights. He remained in Madinah in Shawwāl and Dhū al-Qaʿdah, and in that stay he ransomed most of the prisoners of Quraysh.[^s4.172]

## Names and disputes
Al-Kudr is also called Qarārat al-Kudr. *Al-qarqarah* is a smooth land; *al-kudr* is a bird with a dusky tint in its colours, and the place became known by it, meaning that it is the resting-place of these birds.[^s4.173]

The date is reported differently. Ibn Saʿd places the expedition in the middle of Muḥarram, at the head of twenty-three months; he also gives an expedition of Banī Sulaym on the sixth of Jumādā al-Ūlā at the head of twenty-seven months. Ibn Isḥāq is reported as saying Shawwāl of the second year and Muḥarram of the third year, without the expedition being named for either date.[^s4.172]

The order and number of expeditions before Uḥud also differ. Ibn Isḥāq's order is: Banī Sulaym at al-Kudr, al-Suwayq, Dhū Amr (the expedition of Ghaṭafān), al-Furuʿ from Baḥrān, and Banī Qaynuqāʿ. Ibn Saʿd also counts five but differs in order, placing [[Ghazwat Banī Qaynuqāʿ]] first, on Saturday in the middle of Shawwāl after Badr. Ibn Isḥāq's five are followed by Abū ʿUmar, al-Bayhaqī, Ibn Kathīr, Ibn al-Qayyim and others. *Al-ʿUyūn* distinguishes this expedition from that of Qarqarat al-Kudr and counts six before [[Ghazwat Uḥud]], and *al-Mawrid* follows it.[^s4.172] See also [[Ghazwat al-Suwayq]].
