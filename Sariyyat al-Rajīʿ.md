# Sariyyat al-Rajīʿ (سرية الرجيع)
*The expedition in which ʿĀṣim b. Thābit and his companions were betrayed by Hudhayl, and Khubayb b. ʿAdī and Zayd b. al-Dathinah were killed at al-Tanʿīm*[^s6.41]

The expedition of al-Rajīʿ took place in Ṣafar of the third year.[^s6.39] Companions sent by the Prophet ﷺ were betrayed on the road between ʿUsfān and Makkah by Banū Liḥyān of Hudhayl. [[ʿĀṣim b. Thābit]] ﵁ and others were killed, ʿAbdullāh b. Ṭāriq was killed (at al-Ẓahrān, in one account), and [[Khubayb b. ʿAdī]] ﵁ and Zayd b. al-Dathinah ﵁ were sold in Makkah and killed at al-Tanʿīm.[^s6.41][^s6.42]

## The request and the dispatch

After the killing of Sufyān b. Khālid b. Nubayḥ al-Hudhalī, Banū Liḥyān of Hudhayl went to ʿAḍal and al-Qārah, two tribes, and offered them camels (*farāʾiḍ*) if they would go to the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and ask him to send out a party of his companions to call them to Islam. Their plan was then to kill whom they wished and take the rest to Quraysh in Makkah to obtain a price for them; nothing was dearer to Quraysh than to have one of the companions of Muḥammad brought to them, to mutilate and kill him in retaliation for those of them killed at Badr.[^s6.40][^s6.50]

Seven men of ʿAḍal and al-Qārah came professing Islam and said: "O Messenger of Allāh, there is Islam spread among us, so send with us a party of your companions to have us recite the Qurʾān and to instruct us in Islam." The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ sent seven men with them.[^s6.40] Ibn Saʿd names seven: ʿĀṣim b. Thābit b. Abī al-Aqlaḥ, Marthad b. Abī Marthad, ʿAbdullāh b. Ṭāriq (confederate of Banū Ẓafar), Khubayb b. ʿAdī, Zayd b. al-Dathinah, Khālid b. al-Bukayr and Muʿattib b. ʿUbayd (it is said, b. ʿAwf).[^s6.39] Marthad and his father Kannāz b. al-Ḥuṣayn were both present at Badr; Khubayb was an Awsī Anṣārī present at Badr; Zayd, an Anṣārī of Banū Bayāḍah, was present at Badr and Uḥud; Khālid b. al-Bukayr, of Banū Layth and a confederate of Banū ʿAdī b. Kaʿb, was among the early forerunners and was present at Badr.[^s6.39t]

## The ambush

They went out with the people until they were at al-Hadah between ʿUsfān and Makkah, where the people betrayed them and called for help against them; close to a hundred archers came. In a report in the Ṣaḥīḥ in the Book of Jihād it is close to two hundred men.[^s6.40] The Companions, it is reported, had alighted at al-Rajīʿ near dawn and eaten ʿajwah dates, and a date-stone fell on the ground; they used to travel by night and lie hidden by day. A woman of Hudhayl pasturing sheep saw the stones, found their smallness unfamiliar, said "This is the dates of Yathrib," and cried out to her people. They followed the tracks to a stopping-place where they found date-stones of dates taken as provision from Madīnah, and came after the Companions and found them settled on the mountain.[^s6.40][^bukh4086]

When ʿĀṣim and his companions sensed them they took refuge on a *fadfad* (an overlooking hillock) in a valley called Ghurān, the dwelling-places of Banū Liḥyān. The people surrounded them and said: "You have the covenant and the pledge that if you come down to us we will not kill a man of you; by Allāh, we do not want to kill you, we only want to obtain from you something from the people of Makkah."[^s6.40][^s6.51][^bukh4086] ʿĀṣim said: "As for me, I will not come down under the protection of an unbeliever. O Allāh, I am protecting Your religion for You today, so protect my flesh. O Allāh, inform Your Messenger of us."[^s6.40] Allāh answered ʿĀṣim and informed the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ of the news of ʿĀṣim and his companions on the day they were struck.[^s6.40] They fought them and shot them until they killed ʿĀṣim among seven men with arrows, and Khubayb, Zayd and ʿAbdullāh b. Ṭāriq remained.[^s6.40][^bukh4086]

## ʿĀṣim and the hornets

When ʿĀṣim was killed, Hudhayl wanted to take his head to sell it to Sulāfah bint Saʿd. She had vowed, when her two sons Musāfiʿ and al-Julās, sons of Ṭalḥah b. Abī Ṭalḥah al-ʿAbdarī, were killed — ʿĀṣim had killed them on the day of Uḥud — that if she gained power over his head she would drink wine in his skull; she set a hundred she-camels for whoever brought it. She became Muslim after that.[^s6.41] Allāh sent upon him something like a canopy of *dabr* (hornets and bees), flying in their faces and stinging them, and it protected him so that they could get nothing from him. They said: "Leave him until evening, so that it goes away from him and we can take him." Allāh sent the valley, and the flood carried him off.[^s6.41][^s6.51][^bukh4086] ʿĀṣim ﵁ had given Allāh a covenant that he would not touch a polytheist and no polytheist would touch him; Allāh fulfilled his oath. When the news reached him, ʿUmar b. al-Khaṭṭāb ﵁ used to say: "Allāh protects the believing servant after his death as He protects him in his life."[^s6.41]

Quraysh also sent to ʿĀṣim for some part of his body that they would recognise, for he had killed one of their great men on the day of Badr.[^bukh4086][^s6.41]

## The three and the death of ʿAbdullāh b. Ṭāriq

Khubayb, Zayd and ʿAbdullāh went up the mountain and the enemy could not get at them until they gave them the covenant and the pledge; they came down, and when the enemy had gained power over them they loosed their bowstrings and tied them with them. ʿAbdullāh b. Ṭāriq said: "This is the first betrayal. By Allāh, I will not accompany you; I have in these slain men an example." They dragged him and tried to make him accompany them; he did not, and they killed him.[^s6.41][^bukh4086] In Ibn Isḥāq's account ʿAbdullāh was instead killed on the way to Makkah: when they were at al-Ẓahrān he pulled his hand out of the rope, took his sword, and the people held back from him and pelted him with stones until they killed him; they buried him at al-Ẓahrān.[^s6.41]

The men of Hudhayl went off with Zayd and Khubayb and sold them in Makkah.[^s6.41][^bukh4086] They entered with them in a sacred month, in Dhū al-Qaʿdah, and held them until the sacred months were over.[^s6.41]

## The killing of Zayd b. al-Dathinah

Ṣafwān b. Umayyah bought Zayd to kill him in retaliation for his father Umayyah b. Khalaf; Ṣafwān became Muslim after that.[^s6.42] When the sacred months were over, Ṣafwān sent him with his slave Nisṭās to al-Tanʿīm, outside the Ḥaram, to kill him, and a group of Quraysh gathered, among them Abū Sufyān b. Ḥarb. Abū Sufyān said: "I adjure you by Allāh, O Zayd, would you love that Muḥammad were with us now in your place, that we strike his neck, and that you were with your family?" He said: "By Allāh, I would not love that Muḥammad now, in his place where he is, be pricked by a thorn that hurts him, while I sit among my family." Abū Sufyān said: "I have not seen any of the people love anyone as the companions of Muḥammad love Muḥammad." Then Nisṭās killed him.[^s6.42] Ibn ʿUqbah mentions that Zayd and Khubayb were killed on one day, and that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ was heard, on the day they were killed, saying: "And upon you two be peace."[^s6.42]

## Khubayb in Makkah

Banū al-Ḥārith b. ʿĀmir b. Nawfal bought Khubayb, who had killed al-Ḥārith on the day of Badr.[^bukh4086][^s6.42] He stayed with them as a captive in the house of a woman called Māwiyah, a freedwoman of Ḥujayr b. Abī Ihāb, who became Muslim after that.[^s6.42] One of the daughters of al-Ḥārith said: "I never saw a captive better than Khubayb; I saw him eating from a bunch of grapes when there was no fruit in Makkah that day, and he was bound in iron; it was nothing but provision Allāh provided." Māwiyah likewise said she looked in on him through the crack of the door and saw in his hand a bunch of grapes like a man's head.[^bukh4086][^s6.43] Muḥammad b. ʿUmar adds that he used to pray at night with the Qurʾān and the women would hear him, weep and feel tenderness towards him.[^s6.43]

When they agreed on killing him he borrowed a razor from one of the daughters of al-Ḥārith to shave with, and she lent it to him. A son of hers crept to him and he put him on his thigh with the razor in his hand; she was alarmed, and he said: "Are you afraid I will kill him? I would not do that, if Allāh wills."[^bukh4086] In Māwiyah's account, Khubayb took the razor from the boy who brought it and said: "In our religion we do not hold treachery lawful."[^s6.43] Ibn Ḥajar reconciles the two reports: he asked for the razor from each of the two women, and the son who crept to him was other than the one who brought the iron; and Allāh knows best.[^s6.43]

## The killing of Khubayb

They brought him out in his irons to al-Tanʿīm, and the women, boys, slaves and a group of the people of Makkah went out with him; no one stayed behind, either bereaved of a kinsman and wanting to look upon his revenge, or opposed to Islam and its people. They ordered a long piece of wood and dug for it.[^s6.43] They took him out of the Ḥaram, and he said: "Let me pray two rakʿahs"; then he turned to them and said: "Were it not that you would think what is in me is dismay at death, I would have added." He was the first to establish the two rakʿahs at the time of killing.[^bukh4086][^s6.44] Ibn ʿUqbah says he prayed them at the place of the mosque of al-Tanʿīm.[^s6.44]

Khubayb said: "O Allāh, count them one by one, kill them scattered, and do not leave any of them."[^s6.44] Muʿāwiyah b. Abī Sufyān said that he was present with Abū Sufyān, who threw him to the ground out of fear of Khubayb's supplication. They used to say that if a man is supplicated against and lies down on his side, it passes from him. Ḥuwayṭib b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā, who became Muslim after that, said he put his finger in his ear and ran away so as not to hear it.[^s6.44]

They put him on the wood, turned his face towards Madīnah and bound him fast. They said: "Go back from Islam and we will let you go." He said: "No, by Allāh, I would not love to have gone back from Islam even if I had all that is on the earth." They asked whether he would love Muḥammad in his place while he sat in his house, and he said he would not love that Muḥammad be pricked by a thorn. He said: "O Allāh, I see only the face of an enemy; there is no one here to convey my greeting of peace to Your Messenger, so convey it to him from me."[^s6.44] Muḥammad b. ʿUmar relates from Usāmah b. Zayd ﵄ that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ, among his companions, was taken by the stupor that came with revelation, and said: "And upon him be peace and the mercy of Allāh and His blessings. This is Jibrīl, conveying to me the greeting of peace from Khubayb."[^s6.44][^s6.54] Ibn ʿUqbah says they claimed that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ said that day, while sitting: "And upon you be peace — Khubayb, Quraysh have killed him."[^s6.44]

The polytheists then called forty boys whose fathers had been killed at Badr, gave each a spear and said, "This is the one who killed your fathers," and they stabbed him lightly; he writhed on the wood and turned over so that his face was towards the Kaʿbah, and he said: "Praise be to Allāh, who has put my face towards His qiblah, which He chose for Himself." Then they killed him.[^s6.44] ʿUqbah b. al-Ḥārith stood to him and killed him.[^bukh4086] In Ibn Isḥāq's report, with a sound chain, ʿUqbah said that he was too weak for it, but Abū Maysarah al-ʿAbdarī put the spear in his hand, took his hand with the spear, and he stabbed Khubayb until he killed him.[^s6.45]

Khubayb is reported to have said, as given in al-Bukhārī: "I do not mind, when I am killed a Muslim, on which side my death in Allāh's cause falls; and that is for the sake of the Deity, and if He wills He will bless the limbs of a torn body."[^s6.45][^bukh4086] Other reports say that ʿAmr b. Umayyah came to the wood and untied him and Khubayb fell to the ground, and that when he looked back he saw no trace of him;[^s6.45] and, from al-Ḍaḥḥāk, that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ sent al-Miqdād and al-Zubayr, who found forty intoxicated men around him and took him down; al-Zubayr carried him on his horse, fresh and unchanged, and when the polytheists caught up he threw him down and the earth swallowed him, so he was called "the swallowed of the earth."[^s6.45] Al-Qayrawānī mentions in *Ḥulā al-ʿUlā* that when Khubayb was killed they put his face away from the qiblah and found him facing it; they turned him around repeatedly, then gave up and left him.[^s6.45]

## The hypocrites and the verses

According to Ibn Isḥāq from Ibn ʿAbbās ﵄, when the party of Marthad and ʿĀṣim was struck at al-Rajīʿ, some men of the hypocrites said: "Alas for these killed ones who perished like this: they neither stayed with their families nor fulfilled the message of their master." Concerning that Allāh revealed «وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَنْ يُعْجِبُكَ قَوْلُهُ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا» (al-Baqarah 2:204) and the verses that follow it (2:205–206).[^s6.46] As for 2:207, it is said that it was revealed concerning Ṣuhayb ﵁.[^s6.46]

## Disputes

**Cause and number.** There was disagreement about the cause of the expedition and the number of its men.[^s6.39] Abū Hurayrah, ʿUrwah and Ibn ʿUqbah say that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ sent ten spies to Makkah to bring him news of Quraysh; Ibn Saʿd is certain they were ten and names seven; Muḥammad b. ʿUmar says it is said they were ten; Ibn Isḥāq says six, the seven named less Muʿattib. The apparent position is that the other three were followers and so were not named.[^s6.39] The wording of Muḥammad b. ʿUmar is better in its sequence.[^s6.39]

**Commander.** The Messenger of Allāh ﷺ made Marthad b. Abī Marthad commander; it is said, ʿĀṣim. Al-Ṣāliḥī holds the latter sound, since al-Bukhārī relates it from Abū Hurayrah.[^s6.40][^bukh4086] Ibn Isḥāq says Marthad was martyred in Ṣafar of year three in the expedition of al-Rajīʿ.[^s6.39t]

**Archers.** Al-Ṣāliḥī reconciles the two counts, a hundred and two hundred, by the other hundred not being archers.[^s6.40]

**How the three came down.** In the Ṣaḥīḥ the three came down after receiving the covenant and were then bound; in Ibn Isḥāq's account they softened, desired life and gave their hands, and were taken captive.[^s6.41][^bukh4086]

**The sale.** Muḥammad b. ʿUmar says the first was sold for a *mithqāl* of gold, it is said for fifty *farīḍah*s, and the second for fifty *farīḍah*s, and it is said that people of Quraysh shared in buying him; Ibn Hishām says they were sold in exchange for two captives of Hudhayl.[^s6.41] Zayd was held with some people of Banū Jumaḥ; it is said, with Nisṭās.[^s6.42]

**Who bought Khubayb.** Ibn ʿUqbah, in a report introduced by «زعموا», names among those sharing in the purchase Abū Ihāb b. ʿAzīz, ʿIkrimah b. Abī Jahl, al-Akhnas b. Sharīq, ʿUbaydah b. Ḥakīm, Umayyah b. Abī ʿUtbah, Ṣafwān b. Umayyah and Banū al-Ḥaḍramī.[^s6.42] Ibn Isḥāq says Ḥujayr b. Abī Ihāb al-Tamīmī, confederate of Banū Nawfal, bought him; Ibn Hishām says it was the son of his sister, not the son of his brother, ʿUqbah b. al-Ḥārith b. ʿĀmir, to kill him for his father al-Ḥārith.[^s6.42]

**His killer.** The Ṣaḥīḥ names ʿUqbah b. al-Ḥārith; another wording of Abū Hurayrah's hadith has Abū Surūʿah, whom a group of genealogists identify as the brother of ʿUqbah b. al-Ḥārith. Abū ʿUmar says in *al-Istīʿāb* that Abū Ṣubayrah al-ʿAbdarī killed him with ʿUqbah; the correct name is given as Abū Maysarah, as in Ibn Isḥāq.[^bukh4086][^s6.44][^s6.45]

**Khubayb at Badr.** Abū Hurayrah's hadith says Khubayb killed al-Ḥārith on the day of Badr, and al-Bukhārī relied on it in counting him among the Badr fighters; Ibn Ḥajar calls that well founded. Al-Dimyāṭī objects that the people of the *maghāzī* say that the killer of al-Ḥārith was Khubayb b. Isāf, a Khazrajī, other than Khubayb b. ʿAdī, an Awsī. Ibn Ḥajar replies that this would reject a sound hadith, and offers the possibilities that they killed Khubayb b. ʿAdī for the killing by Khubayb b. Isāf, according to their custom in the Jāhiliyyah, or that Khubayb b. ʿAdī shared in it.[^s6.46]

**First to pray two rakʿahs.** Abū Hurayrah says in the Ṣaḥīḥ that Khubayb was the first to establish the two rakʿahs at killing, and countless people were certain of that. *Al-Ishārah* gives Khubayb first and then says it is said to be Usāmah b. Zayd, when the muleteer wished to betray him; "Usāmah" is the reading of two copies of *al-Ishārah*, and the correct name is Zayd b. Ḥārithah, as in *al-Rawḍ*. The report of Zayd's prayer is not connected in its chain and does not stand against the Ṣaḥīḥ.[^s6.46][^s6.47]

**The verses.** Ibn Isḥāq says 2:204–206 were revealed concerning this expedition; others say concerning al-Akhnas b. Sharīq.[^s6.46]

**The poem.** Ibn Isḥāq's version reads «لعمرك ما آسى إذا مت مسلما»; al-Bukhārī's, «فلست أبالي حين أقتل مسلما», and the Ṣaḥīḥ has «شق», where *Subul al-Hudā* has «جنب».[^s6.45][^bukh4086]
