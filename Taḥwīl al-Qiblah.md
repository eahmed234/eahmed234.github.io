# Taḥwīl al-Qiblah (تحويل القبلة)
*The turning of the prayer from Bayt al-Maqdis to the Kaʿbah*

Taḥwīl al-Qiblah is the turning of the qiblah of the Prophet [[Muḥammad b. ʻAbdullah|Muḥammad b. ʿAbdullāh]] ﷺ from [[Bayt al-Maqdis]] to the Kaʿbah. It came while he was leading the Ẓuhr prayer in a mosque among [[Banū Salimah]], and the mosque was named Masjid al-Qiblatayn for it.[^s3.370] The qiblah was the first thing abrogated of the Qurʾān.[^s3.370]

## Before the turning

In [[Makkah al-Mukarramah|Makkah]] the Prophet ﷺ prayed toward Bayt al-Maqdis with the Kaʿbah between his hands.[^s3.370] When he emigrated to Madīnah, most of whose people were Jews, Allah commanded him to face the rock of Bayt al-Maqdis, and the Jews made insinuations about it.[^s3.370] He liked his qiblah to be toward the House, because the Jews said: "Muḥammad opposes us yet follows our qiblah."[^s3.370][^bukh4486] He said to Jibrīl ﵊: "I wish that Allah, Mighty and Majestic, would turn me from the qiblah of the Jews to another." Jibrīl ﵊ answered: "I am only a servant like you; I have no power over anything for you except what I am commanded; so call upon Allah the Exalted." The Prophet ﷺ then called upon Allah and looked often toward the sky, awaiting His command.[^s3.370] The turning of his face toward the sky was a looking for revelation and a longing for the command to face the Kaʿbah; he wished for it because it is the qiblah of Ibrāhīm and because it was more likely to bring the Arabs to Islam.[^s3.372]

He prayed toward Bayt al-Maqdis for sixteen or seventeen months.[^bukh399][^bukh4486] He prayed toward it to win over the Jews.[^s3.371]

## The turning at Banū Salimah

The Prophet ﷺ went out to visit Umm Bishr, mother of [[Bishr b. al-Barāʾ]] b. Maʿrūr, among Banū Salimah. She made him food, the time of Ẓuhr came, and he led his Companions in the prayer in a mosque there. When he had prayed two rakʿahs, Jibrīl ﵊ descended and signalled to him to pray toward the House, and Jibrīl ﵊ prayed toward the House. The Prophet ﷺ turned around to the Kaʿbah and faced the *mīzāb*, and the women moved to the men's place and the men to the women's place.[^s3.370] This is the qiblah of which Allah said in the verse beginning "We shall surely turn you to a qiblah with which you will be pleased" (2:144).[^s3.370] That day Ẓuhr was four rakʿahs: two toward Bayt al-Maqdis and two toward the Kaʿbah.[^s3.370]

The exchange of places is explained as follows. Whoever faces the Kaʿbah in Madīnah has his back to Bayt al-Maqdis; had the imām turned where he stood there would have been no room behind him for the rows. So the imām moved from the front of the mosque to its rear, the men moved until they were behind him, and the women moved until they were behind the men. This calls for much movement in prayer, and it is possible that it happened before much movement was forbidden, as it happened before speech was forbidden; or that the movement was overlooked for the sake of the benefit; or that the steps were not consecutive but occurred separately.[^s3.372]

## The news in the other mosques

ʿAbbād b. Bishr ﵁, who had prayed with the Prophet ﷺ, went out and passed by a people of the Anṣār at Banū Ḥārithah while they were bowing in the ʿAṣr prayer. He said: "I bear witness by Allah that I prayed with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ toward the House." And they turned around.[^s3.370][^bukh399][^bukh4486] Rāfiʿ b. Khadīj ﵁ said that a comer came to them as they were praying among [[Banū ʿAbd al-Ashhal]] and said that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ had been commanded to face the Kaʿbah; their imām turned them to the Kaʿbah and they turned with him.[^s3.371]

At Qubāʾ, while the people were in the Ṣubḥ prayer, a comer came to them and said that Qurʾān had been revealed to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and that he had been commanded to face the Kaʿbah, so they should face it; their faces were toward Syria, and they turned around to the Kaʿbah.[^s3.371] Ibn Ṭāhir al-Maqdisī says that this comer too was ʿAbbād b. Bishr.[^s3.371]

## Reactions

The Jews and the People of the Book had been pleased that the Prophet ﷺ prayed toward Bayt al-Maqdis; when he turned his face toward the House they rejected it.[^s3.371] The hypocrites said: "Muḥammad has gone back to his land."[^s3.371] The polytheists said: "Muḥammad wanted to make us a qiblah and a means for himself; he has recognised that our religion is better guided than his, and he is about to be on our religion."[^s3.371] The Jews said to the believers: "What has turned you from the qiblah of Mūsā, Yaʿqūb and the qiblah of the prophets? By Allah, you are only a people being put to trial."[^s3.371] The believers said: "People of ours have gone — they died — and we do not know whether we and they were on a qiblah or not."[^s3.371]

Rifāʿah b. Qays, Kardam b. ʿAmr, Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf, Rāfiʿ b. Abī Rāfiʿ, al-Ḥajjāj b. ʿAmr (an ally of Kaʿb b. al-Ashraf), and al-Rabīʿ and Kinānah, sons of al-Rabīʿ b. Abī al-Ḥuqayq, came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: "Muḥammad, what has turned you from your qiblah, which you were on, while you claim to be on the creed and religion of Ibrāhīm? Return to your qiblah that you were on and we will follow you and believe you." What they wanted by that was to turn him away from his religion.[^s3.371]

## The revelation in Sūrat al-Baqarah

Allah then sent down the verses of [[Sūrat al-Baqarah]] beginning "The foolish among the people will say…".[^s3.371] The foolish are the ignorant: the Jews, the polytheists and the hypocrites; the qiblah from which they say the believers were turned is Bayt al-Maqdis; and to Allah belong all directions, so He commands facing whichever He wills; He guides whom He wills to a straight path, which is the religion of Islam (2:142).[^s3.371][^bukh399] The community of Muḥammad is made a middle community, the best and upright, to be witnesses over the people on the Day of Resurrection that their messengers conveyed to them, with the Messenger a witness over it (2:143).[^s3.371] The qiblah the Prophet ﷺ had been on was appointed only that it might be made manifest who follows the Messenger and believes him and who turns back on his heels, that is, returns to disbelief in doubt of the religion; a group apostatised for that, and the turning was a great matter except for those whom Allah guided.[^s3.371][^s3.372]

The verse "We have certainly seen the turning of your face in the sky" commands him to face al-Masjid al-Ḥarām, that is the Kaʿbah, and the community, wherever it is, to face it in prayer; those given the Book know it to be the truth from their Lord because of what is in their books of the description of the Prophet ﷺ that he will turn toward it (2:144).[^s3.372][^bukh399] Were he to bring every sign they would not follow his qiblah out of obstinacy; he will not follow theirs, and the Jews do not follow the qiblah of the Christians nor the Christians that of the Jews (2:145).[^s3.372]

## Those who died before the turning

The believers' question about those who had died before the turning is the cause of the revelation of the words that Allah would never have caused their faith to be lost, that is their prayer toward Bayt al-Maqdis, for which He rewards them (2:143).[^s3.372] In al-Barāʾ's report, those who died on the qiblah before it was turned toward the House were men who were killed, and the Companions did not know what to say about them, so Allah sent down this verse.[^bukh4486]

Ten Muslims died before the prayer was made obligatory and before the turning of the qiblah. In Makkah, of Quraysh: ʿAbdullāh b. Shihāb and al-Muṭṭalib b. Azhar, both of Zuhrah, and al-Sakrān b. ʿAmr al-ʿĀmirī. In Abyssinia: Ḥaṭṭāb b. al-Ḥārith al-Jumaḥī, ʿAmr b. Umayyah al-Asadī, ʿAbdullāh b. al-Ḥārith al-Sahmī, and ʿUrwah b. ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā and ʿAdī b. Naḍlah, both of ʿAdī. Of the Anṣār in Madīnah: [[al-Barāʾ b. Maʿrūr]] and [[Asʿad b. Zurārah]]. These ten are agreed upon; Iyās b. Muʿādh al-Ashhalī also died in that period, but his Islam is disputed.[^s3.374]

## Disputes

**Duration.** Al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib gave sixteen or seventeen months;[^bukh399][^bukh4486] Ibn ʿAbbās, in Ibn Isḥāq and in Abū Dāwūd's *Nāsikh*, and ʿAmr b. ʿAwf gave seventeen; Ibn ʿAbbās also, with al-Zuhrī, Saʿīd b. al-Musayyab, Qatādah and others, gave sixteen; Anas b. Mālik gave nineteen.[^s3.372][^s3.373] Another report of al-Barāʾ, in Ibn Mājah, has eighteen months, the qiblah being turned two months after the entry into Madīnah.[^s3.373] Al-Ḥāfiẓ reconciles sixteen, seventeen and the doubt between them: whoever was sure of sixteen pieced together the month of arrival, the month of the turning and a month, and dropped the extra days; whoever was sure of seventeen counted both; and whoever doubted hesitated. He holds the chains of thirteen, eighteen, nineteen and ten months, of two months and of two years to be weak, and reliance to be on the first three. Ibn Ḥibbān's "seventeen months and three days" is built on the arrival having been on the second of Rabīʿ al-Awwal.[^s3.373]

**Month.** According to al-Ḥāfiẓ, the arrival was in Rabīʿ al-Awwal without disagreement, and the turning was after midday in the middle of Rajab of the second year on the sound view, which the majority are certain of and which al-Ḥākim reports with a sound chain from Ibn ʿAbbās.[^s3.373] Muḥammad b. Ḥabīb said the middle of Shaʿbān, which al-Nawawī mentioned in *al-Rawḍah* and approved, although in his commentary on Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim he preferred the report of sixteen months because Muslim states it with certainty; Shaʿbān is not coherent except by dropping the two months of arrival and of the turning. Mūsā b. ʿUqbah was certain that it was in Jumādā al-Ākhirah.[^s3.373]

**Which prayer.** In the Ṣaḥīḥ, from al-Barāʾ b. ʿĀzib, the first prayer the Prophet ﷺ prayed was ʿAṣr; most hold it was Ẓuhr. Al-Ḥāfiẓ's verified position is that the first prayer he prayed among Banū Salimah was Ẓuhr, the first he prayed in the Prophet's mosque was ʿAṣr, and Ṣubḥ was for the people of Qubāʾ.[^s3.373]

**Praying toward Bayt al-Maqdis in Makkah.** There is disagreement about it. The report of al-Barāʾ in Ibn Mājah apparently means that he prayed in Makkah toward Bayt al-Maqdis alone. Al-Zuhrī related a disagreement over whether he put the Kaʿbah behind his back, in which case he put the *mīzāb* behind him, or between himself and Bayt al-Maqdis, in which case he prayed between the two Yamānī corners. Some claimed he had always faced the Kaʿbah in Makkah, faced Bayt al-Maqdis on coming to Madīnah, and was then abrogated; al-Ḥāfiẓ calls this weak, since it requires abrogation twice, and prefers the first of the two views because it combines the two statements; al-Ḥākim and others have declared it sound. Abū ʿUmar took it in the second sense, and Jibrīl's leading the prayer at the House supports taking it at its apparent sense.[^s3.373][^s3.374] A report of Ibn ʿAbbās with a good, strong chain says that when the Prophet ﷺ emigrated to Madīnah Allah commanded him to face Bayt al-Maqdis, which apparently means that facing it began after the emigration; Aḥmad reports from Ibn ʿAbbās by another route that he prayed in Makkah toward Bayt al-Maqdis with the Kaʿbah between his hands, and Ibn Saʿd also reports it with a good, strong chain. The two can be combined if, on emigrating, he was commanded to continue praying toward Bayt al-Maqdis.[^s3.374] The words "Allah commanded him" refute the saying that he prayed toward Bayt al-Maqdis by *ijtihād*, reported by Ibn Jarīr from ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Zayd b. Aslam, who is weak; and Abū al-ʿĀliyah's report that he did so to win over the People of the Book is not fitting except by divine direction.[^s3.374] Ibn Jurayj is reported as saying that the Prophet ﷺ first prayed toward the Kaʿbah, was then turned to Bayt al-Maqdis in Makkah, and prayed three years before emigrating.[^s3.370]

**The men who were killed.** The mention of killing occurs, according to al-Ḥāfiẓ, only in al-Zuhrī's report; the other reports mention death alone, and he found no report that any Muslim was killed before the turning, though absence of mention does not entail that it did not happen. If the wording is preserved, it means that some Muslim not well known was killed in that period, not in jihād, and it was not recorded because little attention was then given to dating. He found in the history a man whose Islam is disputed, Suwayd b. al-Ṣāmit, who may be meant; and it was suggested to him that the weak killed in Makkah, such as the parents of [[ʿAmmār b. Yāsir]], might be meant, to which he replied that it must first be established that they were killed after the Isrāʾ.[^s3.374][^s3.375]
